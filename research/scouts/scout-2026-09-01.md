## 1. The commission

**CEO focus, as given:** an idea that fills a gap in the market and solves a real problem for everyday people or businesses — particularly where following Candour's constitution would mean shipping for significantly less money in an area that normally charges extortionate amounts when it doesn't need to.

**CEO calibration, collected before generation:**

| Dimension | CEO's answer |

| Customer | Everyday consumers; micro-businesses & sole traders; small orgs (charities, clubs, schools, care) |

The grievance was treated as the primary scouting signal. Section 5 reports what happened to it, which is the most important result in this document.

## 2. Method, and its honest limits

1. CVO generated nine candidates against the calibration above.
2. Three Research Analysts ran a light validation pass in parallel — roughly an hour's rigour per candidate, not a research brief. Retrieved sources only; citations from memory prohibited per `pipeline/evidence-standard.md`.
3. The Skeptic culled cold, with web access, and was instructed to attack the CVO's own cross-cutting thesis.

**Limits a reader should hold against this document:**

- All evidence is secondary. Candour has no industry contacts, so there are no interviews and no observed user behaviour behind any conclusion here. [E-standard, §"Honesty about our evidence situation"]
- A large share of search results in these markets was AI-generated SEO blogspam written by competitors. The analysts discarded it and re-retrieved primary sources; two spark theses were found to be sourced from a competitor's marketing blog and were corrected (see B and E).
- **The Skeptic records that its invocation was not clean** — it was handed nine pre-labelled verdicts, a ranking, and two named survivors before it looked. That anchoring is on the record in its memo and the CEO should weight it. It moved two of three rankings against the anchor, which is some evidence the anchoring did not bind.
- One load-bearing kill (H) rests on a **single law-firm summary** of an Ofgem decision; the underlying supply licence condition text was not retrieved. Flagged in section 3 and in the Skeptic's falsifiability table.

Full Skeptic memo, published unedited: [`scout-2026-09-01-skeptic-cull.md`](scout-2026-09-01-skeptic-cull.md).

## 4. Three patterns, not nine failures

**Pattern 1 — where cost-to-serve is genuinely near zero, someone has already competed the price to £0.** A, G and I died this way. This is the most direct refutation of the founding intuition and it recurred every time the intuition was applied literally.

**Pattern 2 — regulation is a countdown, not a moat.** The CVO's response to pattern 1 was to hunt markets where a low cost-to-serve is protected by regulation, liability, trust, channel control or conflict of interest. The Skeptic dismantled this and the CVO accepts the dismantling. H's protection was a conflict of interest — and regulators exist to remove conflicts of interest; Ofgem removed exactly this one, for exactly this segment, in exactly this output format, in 2022. B's protection was mandatory compliance demand — which is precisely *why* a label vendor gives the software away, because guaranteed demand is worth more as a customer-acquisition channel than as a licence fee. Three of nine candidates were killed, or are scheduled to be killed, by a regulator doing its job.

**Pattern 3 — distribution killed everything the first two patterns missed.** It was not on the CEO's constraint list and it should have been. But the fix is not a distribution filter bolted onto the end of ideation: a channel that is *validatable from public sources* is by definition a channel every competitor can also see. F is the proof — its channel scored highest of all nine and is a public directory of 38 rural charities.

## 5. What happened to the grievance

The MyFitnessPal grievance did not survive, and it is worth being precise about why, because the reasoning generalises.

MFP's UK price is £64.99/yr Premium (£79.99 Premium+) [[E](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/premium)], against a cost-to-serve of pennies per user per year. That gap is real. But it is a fact about **MyFitnessPal's scale**, not about its dishonesty: cost-plus-labour at millions of users is a tiny number, and the difference between that number and £64.99 is margin earned by having millions of users. Candour, at a few hundred users, would have a *higher* honest cost per user, not a lower one.

And the market has already produced the honest version: Cronometer gives away barcode scanning and a million-item database at £0. What remains of the grievance, once that is on the table, is a preference for products without ads and upsells. That is a legitimate taste. It is not a pricing wedge, and the CVO was wrong to treat it as one.

## 6. CVO response to the dissent

Constitution 5.3 requires responding to dissent in writing rather than outvoting it. This is not a gate, but the same duty applies.

**Accepted in full:**

- All nine kills. The CVO does not put forward a survivor. Manufacturing one to make the round look productive would be the exact dishonesty this company exists to avoid.
- The correction to the CVO's cross-cutting thesis (pattern 2 above). It was wrong. The narrower version the Skeptic endorses — *channel control and trust are real protections; regulation and conflict-of-interest are not* — is adopted.
- That candidate B should have been screened out at intake on the CEO's own stated constraints, and that ranking it first was a process failure by the CVO, not a judgement call. A defective allergen label has a plausible path to a death, and Constitution 6.1 would have mandated solicitor review and PI insurance — fixed costs that break the CEO's own <£30/mo ceiling.
- The Article 9 finding: perpetual *scouting* achieves perpetual planning while sitting entirely outside the 5.2 clock. The loophole is real, it is now visible, and Article 9 commits the company to naming rather than using it.

**Partly disputed — one point, and it matters:**

The Skeptic's arithmetic treats 0.4 FTE-year of build labour as a **year-one operating cost**, producing a required revenue of ~£27,750/yr for candidate F. The CVO's position is that build labour is capital, not run-rate, and the defensible treatment amortises it over the product's expected life (3–5 years) alongside ongoing support costed separately. On that reading F's published annual cost is roughly £5,000–£8,000, not £23,126.

This does not rescue F — at 30 customers that is still £170–£270 per hall against a £129 incumbent — and the Skeptic's conclusion therefore stands. But the magnitude is materially different, and **the constitution does not currently define which treatment applies.** "Cost" is a defined term under Article 9, changeable only by public amendment. Defining it now, while no product exists and nothing turns on the answer, is the honest moment to do it. Defining it later, when a price depends on it, is exactly the gaming vector Article 9 lists as "inflating costs to raise the allowable price". This belongs to the CFO seat and should be settled before round two.

**One implication the CVO draws that the dissent did not:**

The cost model is dominated by founder labour, not infrastructure. The constitution therefore does not forbid low-volume products — it forbids **labour-heavy** low-volume products. A tool built in two or three weeks with near-zero support load carries an honest cost of roughly £3–4k/yr, which at 30 customers is ~£130 each: competitive with real incumbents. A five-month build at the same volume is not, and never becomes so.

That converts Constitution 1.5 and the CVO charter's "smallest honest version" question from a stylistic preference into a hard economic screen, and it points at a target profile none of the nine matched:

- Build effort measured in **weeks**, not months.
- Incumbent prices set by **human** cost-to-serve — brokers, consultants, agencies, hourly professional work — rather than by scale-subsidised software. Software cost-plus at low volume can undercut a human, and cannot undercut a subsidised platform.
- A channel that **exists before the product**, not one identified after it.

The CEO should note that the second of these collides with the constraint "avoid regulated / liability-heavy domains" — much human-priced work is human-priced *because* it is regulated. That contradiction is unresolved and belongs to the CEO.

## 7. Recommended changes to the method, before round two

1. **Cost sheet before research, not after.** Use `pipeline/templates/cost-sheet.md` at intake: a worked Candour price at honest year-one volume, against the retrieved incumbent price. The Skeptic's judgement is that this alone would have killed at least six of the nine in an afternoon. The CVO agrees.
2. **Search from the channel, not from the idea.** Start with "who can the CEO reach repeatedly without paid acquisition?" and look for a product inside that answer. If the honest answer is "nobody yet", that is the finding, and the correct next action is to build reach rather than to generate a tenth candidate.
3. **Screen against the CEO's stated constraints at intake.** B reached a research analyst despite failing two of them.
4. **Add a build-effort ceiling** to the intake screen, expressed in weeks, per section 6.
5. **Adopt a stopping rule for scouting** so the 5.2 loophole is closed by cadence rather than by good intentions.

## 8. Decisions for the CEO (Constitution 5.4 — none of these are the CVO's to take)

1. **Promote nothing, or overrule.** The CVO recommends promoting none of the nine. F is the only one where an overrule would be defensible, and the Skeptic has recorded the objection a brief would have to answer and the fact that would kill it.
2. **Define how build labour is costed** (capital amortised over product life, or year-one operating cost). Article 9 term; CFO seat prepares, CEO decides, public amendment if it changes a definition.
3. **Resolve the contradiction** between "avoid regulated/liability-heavy domains" and any strategy of hunting human-priced markets. The CVO recommends keeping the constraint and rejecting the thesis.
4. **Rule on the Article 9 addition** — whether "perpetual scouting evades the 5.2 clock" is added to the loophole table, and whether a scouting cadence cap is adopted.
5. **Rule on method changes 1–5** in section 7.

## 9. What round two should look like

Not a tenth candidate. In order: settle decision 2 (cost definition), then run a channel-first pass — what audiences can this company actually reach today — and only then generate ideas inside whatever that produces, screened by a worked cost sheet before any analyst time is spent. If the channel pass returns nothing, the honest output is a public statement of that, not more ideas.
