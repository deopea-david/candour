## 1. The cull

**A — honest-food-tracker. Dead.** Verified: Cronometer's Basic tier includes barcode scanning at £0, with Gold at $4.99/mo annual as the upsell (https://cronometer.com/gold/) [E]. The honest product ships free today. Nothing further needed.

**C — mtd-itsa-filing. Dead.** Verified on HMRC's own recognition register: "My Tax Digital (Free)" is listed free, ready now, covering sole trader business income, UK and foreign property income, and agent use (https://www.tax.service.gov.uk/find-making-tax-digital-income-tax-software/product-details?productId=3255) [E]. The entire target user is served, free, by an HMRC-recognised product.

**D — council-tax-band-check. Dead.** Verified: 39,590 challenges resolved 2023-24, 27% reduced, under 1% increased, and volumes falling — challenges received fell 51,300 → 43,820 (https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/council-tax-challenges-and-changes-in-england-and-wales-march-2024/council-tax-challenges-and-changes-statistical-summary) [E]. A shrinking one-shot market with free public tooling. Separately, and sufficient on its own: a successful challenge can re-band a non-consenting neighbour's home upward. Constitution 1.2 forbids profiting from harm imposed on people who never bought anything from us. That is a principled kill, not an economic one, and it should be recorded as such.

**E — smallbiz-compliance-calendar. Dead.** Verified: the ABI/Public First study (n=1,002 SME decision-makers) attributes underinsurance to awareness gaps, affordability and failure to *review* cover — and does not mention missed deadlines or compliance calendars anywhere (https://www.publicfirst.co.uk/smallbusbigrisk) [E]. The one piece of evidence cited for the pain explicitly measures a different pain.

**G — honest-document-toolkit. Dead.** I did not re-verify; I don't need to. The analyst's own finding — that PDF→Word cannot run client-side — means the privacy pitch fails at exactly the one operation people pay for. A product whose differentiator switches off at the point of monetisation is not a product.

**I — subscription-audit-no-openbanking. Dead, twice over.** Verified: Snoop's free tier explicitly includes "Regular payment and subscription tracking," "Weekly reminder of upcoming bills" and "Contract renewal reminders," free forever (https://www.snoop.app/plus) [E]. The core feature is free today, and the DMCCA regime is scheduled to make renewal reminders a legal obligation on traders. Killed by a competitor and then by Parliament.

**H — business-energy-contract-decoder. Dead, and the analyst had it backwards.** This is the correction that matters most in the cull. The analyst treated Ofgem's 1 Oct 2024 extension as the thing that "largely closed the gap." It is not. Ofgem's Microbusiness Strategic Review decision (28 March 2022) already required, **from 1 October 2022**, that brokerage costs be disclosed to microbusinesses via the Principal Terms, both pre- and post-contract, and — decisively — "must be presented as a total cost in pounds/pence covering the duration of the supply contract" (https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/ofgem-publishes-its-decision-on-the-microbusiness-strategic-review) [E, single source: a law-firm summary of the Ofgem decision; I did not retrieve the SLC text itself, and I flag that]. Corroborated on direction by Ofgem's own release, which states only microbusinesses were previously covered and that October 2024 extended it to all non-domestic customers (https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/ofgem-confirms-greater-protection-businesses) [E].

Read that against the product. H proposed to take a micro-business energy contract and output the embedded broker commission as a pound figure over the contract term. That output has been a mandatory supply licence condition, delivered by the supplier, before signature, in that exact format, for nearly four years. The 2024 change extended weaker p/kWh disclosure to *larger* firms — the segment Candour is not targeting. Candour's target user is the one segment where the gap was closed first and closed best. The Energy Ombudsman figures are real (1,568 cases, +112%, 58% upheld, 88% sales-related, signposting 5%→8%, "unacceptably low" — https://www.energyombudsman.org/news/energy-ombudsman-report-on-second-year-of-broker-scheme) [E] — but they evidence a *redress signposting* failure, not a *disclosure* failure. Those are different problems, and the product addressed the one that is already solved.

**B — allergen-labelling-micro-food. Dead. Three independent reasons, any one sufficient.**

*(i) The evidence measures the wrong thing.* I retrieved the underlying FSA publication the analyst could not (https://science.food.gov.uk/article/162217-retail-surveillance-survey-sampling-programme-2025-26) [E]. It is the Retail Surveillance Survey 2025/26: 845 products bought from shops, ~60% smaller businesses / 40% large retailers, 64% of small-retailer samples satisfactory vs 82% large. The non-compliance driving that gap is Dubai-style chocolate with missing durability dates and absent UK importer details, goat meat that was sheep, coffee composition, glycerol in slush drinks, aflatoxins and cadmium. It is a survey of *packaged goods on shelves*, overwhelmingly imported or manufactured, and the FSA publication does not treat PPDS-made-on-premises as a category at all. Labelling software fixes none of it. You cannot fix meat substitution with a label generator. The single statistic carrying this candidate is not about the user, the product, or the problem.

*(ii) The price is already £0, and structurally so.* Positive ID Labels gives its Nutridata allergen labelling software away free to anyone who buys labels from it, with a paid Pro tier only for nutrition data (https://pid-labelling.co.uk/free-allergy-labelling-software/) [E]. This is a razor-and-blades subsidy. It is a *harder* free competitor than an ad-funded app or an open-source project, because a consumables business can fund the software indefinitely and has a positive reason to keep it free forever. Candour sells no labels.

*(iii) It breaks two stated CEO constraints and should have been screened out at intake.* The CEO said avoid regulated/liability-heavy domains and hold fixed cost under £30/mo. A defective allergen label has a path to a death; Constitution 6.1 mandates qualified human review before launch; the analyst itself concluded solicitor review and PI insurance would be required. That is a regulated liability domain with an insurance line item. Ranking it #1 of its trio was a process failure, not a judgement call — the intake screen should never have passed it to research.

## 3. Verdict on the CVO's thesis and the distribution screen

**On the thesis — it is half right, and the wrong half is load-bearing.**

The observation is sound: A, G and I did die the same death, and where cost-to-serve is near zero someone usually has competed the price to £0. The *inference* — that Candour should therefore hunt markets where low cost-to-serve is protected by regulation, liability, trust, channel control or conflict of interest — is where it fails, and this batch is the disproof.

The two candidates that survived to the CVO's shortlist survived *because* of that protection. Both are now dead, and both died from the protection itself:

- **H's protection was a conflict of interest.** Regulators exist to remove conflicts of interest. Ofgem removed this one, for this exact customer segment, in this exact output format, in October 2022. A conflict of interest is not a moat; it is a queue for a regulator.
- **B's protection was liability plus regulation.** The regulation created a permanent, mandatory, universal demand — which is precisely why a consumables vendor gave the software away free to sell labels against it. Regulation didn't protect the margin. **Regulation manufactured a captive market that someone with an adjacent revenue stream was delighted to serve at £0.** Mandated demand attracts subsidy, because a guaranteed audience is worth more as a customer acquisition channel than as a software licence.

So the corrected finding is: *regulation and conflict-of-interest are not protection. They are a countdown.* Regulation creates the niche and then closes it, on a published timetable, and the timetable is public — which means anyone can see the window and so can Candour's would-be competitors. I already have a third instance in this batch: candidate I dies partly because the DMCCA will legislate its value away on a known date. Three of nine candidates were killed or will be killed by a regulator doing its job. That is the pattern, and it points the opposite way from the thesis.

There is a narrower version of the CVO's thesis I *would* endorse: **channel control and trust are real protections; regulation and conflict-of-interest are not.** Note that the two survivable protections are the two Candour does not have and cannot buy cheaply.

**On the distribution screen — the analyst is right about the disease and wrong about the cure.**

"What kills all three is distribution" is correct, and it is the honest summary of all nine. But adding a distribution screen to the front of this method will not fix it, because a distribution channel that is *validatable from public sources* is by definition a channel visible to everyone. F is the proof: the ACRE directory passed the analyst's implicit distribution test with the highest mark of the nine, and it is a public list of 38 charities. The screen would have waved it through.

**Rule on it:** adopt the screen, but not as a filter at the end of ideation. Reverse the search order. Candour's own evidence standard already states plainly that the company has no industry contacts. If that is true — and it is the CEO's own stated constraint — then the binding constraint on this company is reach, not ideas, and generating ideas before establishing reach is solving the abundant problem. Scouting should start from *"what group of people can the CEO actually reach, repeatedly, without paid acquisition?"* and search for a product inside that answer. If the honest answer is "none," that is the finding, and no amount of further ideation changes it.

## 4. The thing nobody has said

**Candour's honest-pricing rule is a scale weapon, and Candour has no scale. At zero scale, honest cost-plus pricing produces a price that is *higher* than the incumbent's "exploitative" one — for every candidate on this list.**

Nine candidates were assessed and not one Candour price was computed. That is the missing artifact, and computing it changes the whole exercise.

Constitution 2.1 requires labour to be costed at a stated market benchmark "whether or not it is actually drawn," and prices to target ~20% over published cost, hard-capped at 30%. Take the median UK full-time software developer salary, £56,914 (ONS ASHE Table 14, April 2025 provisional) [E, single source — reported via https://payprecision.co.uk/salaries/software-developer/ citing ASHE; I did **not** retrieve the ONS table itself and this figure should be upgraded before anyone relies on it].

Apply it to F, the strongest candidate [I, from the above [E] premises; assumptions stated and contestable]:

- 0.4 FTE-year of founder time on build and support: **£22,766**
- Hosting and tooling at the CEO's own ceiling, £30/mo: **£360**
- Published annual cost: **£23,126**
- At the 2.1 target of +20%: **required revenue ≈ £27,750/yr**

At Village Hall Hub's entry price of £129/yr, that is **215 paying halls**. At Hallmaster's booking-and-invoicing price of £279/yr, **100 halls**. Against ~10,000 English village halls and six-plus incumbents, in year one, with no channel. The alternative is to price honestly at achievable volume: 30 customers implies **£925/yr**, which is 7× Village Hall Hub and 3.3× Hallmaster's single-venue top tier.

That is the whole problem in one line. **The incumbent's price is low because it is subsidised by scale the incumbent already has. Candour's price is high because honesty at zero scale is expensive.** MyFitnessPal's £64.99/yr is not high relative to cost-plus-labour at a few hundred users — it is high relative to cost-plus-labour at millions of users, which is a fact about *MyFitnessPal's scale*, not about its dishonesty. The founding grievance mistakes an economy of scale for an ethical failing. What the grievance actually contains, once Cronometer is on the table at £0, is a preference for no ads and no upsell. That is a legitimate taste. It is not a market, and it does not generalise into a pricing wedge.

**And the constitutional trap closes behind it.** Two clauses interlock:

- **2.1, final sentence:** labour is benchmarked "so unpaid effort never fakes a low price." Launching F at £129 with a published cost of £23,126 is *exactly* unpaid effort faking a low price. It is not a "justified deviation"; it is the named thing the clause exists to prevent. Article 2.1 does not merely fail to help here — it forbids the standard playbook.
- **2.3, step 3.1:** surplus goes first to price reductions for existing customers. So the price ratchets down and can never come back up. An underpriced launch is permanent, by constitution.

Together these mean Candour must set its launch price at the scale it *hopes* to reach, publish a cost sheet showing a large loss until it gets there, and never recover the price if it doesn't. That is a coherent choice for a company that has decided to subsidise customers out of founder time — but it is the opposite of the founding intuition, it has never been written down, and **no candidate on this list should be assessed again until it has been.** I would put a worked cost sheet, at honest year-one volume, into the intake screen ahead of everything else. It is cheaper than a research brief and it would have killed at least six of these nine on the first afternoon.

**A second, smaller finding, and I raise it because Article 9 invites it.** Constitution 5.2 starts its four-week clock at the research brief. Nine candidates have been generated, assessed, ranked and debated, and not one clock is running — correctly, by the letter of the rule. But 5.2 exists "to force building over perpetual planning," and perpetual *scouting* achieves perpetual planning while sitting entirely outside 5.2's reach. This is a gaming vector against the rule's stated purpose, it is not in the Article 9 table, and Article 9 commits the company to adding such vectors rather than using them. I am not alleging bad faith — this is the first scouting round. I am saying the loophole is now visible and the commitment is to name it. My suggestion for the CEO, as a decision that is his alone: a cap on scouting rounds before something must enter the pipeline or the search premise must be publicly revised.

**Last, on the CEO's own constraints.** "Avoid regulated/liability-heavy domains" and the CVO's thesis "go where regulation protects the margin" are in direct contradiction. Adopting the thesis means overturning the constraint. Nobody has noticed. Whichever way that resolves, it is a CEO decision and it should be made explicitly rather than by drift — and my recommendation is to resolve it by rejecting the thesis, for the reasons in section 3, and keeping the constraint.
