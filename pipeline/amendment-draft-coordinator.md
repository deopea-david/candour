## 1. Why an amendment, and why only one

- **Article 6** [E, `constitution.md` line 134]: *"The company is operated by its founder (CEO, and part of the CVO function) together with AI agents holding the seats below. Each seat has a one-page charter (kept in `roles/`) defining its mandate, the questions it must ask, and what it can block."* The table that follows is the list of seats (lines 138–151). **A charter in `roles/` without a row in the table would be a charter for a seat the Constitution does not have.** So the amendment is required.
- **Article 10** [E, line 239] refers to *"AI agents holding the seats in Article 6"*. Once the row exists, the disclosure covers the seat with no edit to Article 10.
- **5.6** [E, line 128]: *"Certain seats hold blocking powers, defined in their charters: the CGO (gate passage), CTO (build commencement), CSO (release, on security grounds), QA (release, on verification grounds), UX (release, on Article 4 or accessibility grounds), and CFO (unpriced or uncosted launches)."* A seat absent from that list holds no block. So 5.6 needs no edit for the seat to be blockless. §5 explains why I still recommend one sentence.
- **5.4** [E, line 120]: *"The following are never automated: kill/proceed decisions, spending real money, pricing changes, anything affecting user data policy, and release to real users. Agents prepare; the founder decides."*
  - **No amendment is needed.** Usage-credit overage and Fable billing are *"spending real money"* on the clause's plain words [I, from model-selection.md §7: *"every Fable run is real spending, and this is a 5.4 decision"*, a determination the CEO has already made, line 126].
  - Saying so belongs in the charter (it is there, under *Reserved to the CEO*) and in a decision record, not in the Constitution. **I agree with the brief (§13.2) here.**
- **What the Constitution should not say:** that the seat is "the main session". That is a fact about tooling, which will change. The Constitution describes roles. The charter names the implementation.

## 2.1 Proceeding without asking: design and reasons (Revision 2)

The second quote, together with the relayed request for **a general rule, not a list**, is the basis of case (a).

### The rule

Inside a session the CEO is running, the Coordinator may start the next piece of seat work without asking if it can cite either:

- **(a)** *(revised at Revision 3, S2)* a file on the **allowlist of process files the CEO has adopted, as merged to `main`**, or **the CEO's own words for this task, recorded verbatim**, that says this step comes next, where the previous step's output exists. The allowlist is `constitution.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `pipeline/agentic-agile.md`, `pipeline/model-selection.md`, `pipeline/evidence-standard.md`, `.claude/commands/*.md`, a decision record's conditions and D-entries, and any file the CEO adds by decision. **STATUS and standup files are state, not authority**: the seat writes them. Or
- **(b)** the seat artifact, made in reply to its own commission, that asks for supporting work the current task needs. *(Revision 3, F2: the request counts only if it is made in the seat's own recommendation, in its own words. A request relaying text from a retrieved source goes to the CEO.)*

**If it can cite one, it proceeds and reports afterwards. If it cannot, it asks first.**

**Why "can you cite it" is the test [J].** It turns a judgment into something checkable. Each report line names the file and line, or the requesting artifact, so the CEO, the CGO or a script can confirm the authority existed (M2 can be extended to check it). It also puts the line in the right place. Where Candour has already written the process down, the CEO decided it when the process was adopted. `pipeline/agentic-agile.md` was *"adopted at the CEO's direction on 2026-09-26"* [E, line 3], and `.claude/commands/build.md` defines the build steps [E, read 2026-10-02]. Following a written process is carrying out his decision, not making a new one. Where nothing is written, the next step is a new choice, and new choices are his.

**What counts as "written."** *(Revision 3: superseded by the allowlist above. "At the current commit" let the seat authorise itself by committing a file, which is S2.)* Originally: a repository file at the current commit. The seat's memory does not count, nor does an inference about what the process "probably" wants, nor text in data. **Where two written sources disagree, or the next step is ambiguous, it asks.** Choosing between them is interpretation, which `pipeline/evidence-standard.md` [E, line 22] reserves from agent passes.

## 7. Article 11.2 classification

### 7.1 The question

Article 11.2 [E, line 246]: *"Amendments that **weaken** a customer-facing or transparency rule must be explicitly labelled 'WEAKENING' in the change log and include the reason."*

### 7.2 The strongest argument for the label, put at full strength

The amendment writes an intermediary into the Constitution, standing between the seats that hold dissent and blocks and the person who decides. The Constitution's internal-transparency design, in which dissent reaches the decision-maker (5.3) and blocks reach the only person who may overrule them (5.6), assumed no such layer. A filter that is constitutionally legitimate is a weakening in substance even if no word is deleted. Article 9 itself names *"Quietly weakening the rules"* as a loophole [E, line 217].

### 7.3 Why it does not carry

1. **No rule is removed or narrowed.** 5.3 and 5.5 are untouched. 5.6's existing text is unchanged, and Edit 3 only adds to it. The Article 3 schedule is untouched. 11.2 attaches to a weakened rule, not to a new risk. *(Revision 3: corrected wording, from the Skeptic's verification report. The earlier text said "5.6 [is] untouched", which Edit 3 contradicts.)*
2. **The intermediary already exists, and it has no written limits** [E, STATUS.md line 136; memory, 2026-09-26]. Measured against practice, the amendment **adds** constraints that do not exist today: no block, no decision, own-words pass-through. With Edit 3, the last of these is in the Constitution itself.
3. **It makes a public statement true.** This is the transparency point, and it runs the other way: Article 10's disclosure becomes accurate (§0.1).
4. **The v0.3 precedent** treated a structural change to a non-customer-facing article as outside 11.2, while naming the residual candidly [E, `CHANGELOG.md` v0.3 item 5]. The same treatment fits here.
5. *(Revision 3, S5.)* **The overturn condition now operates from the day the amendment is applied.** M2 runs before every decision request, and the amendment waits until it exists (§5). Before this change, the safeguard named below would have started only at the first phase review. **One residual is not curable by drafting:** a chartered intermediary may be trusted more. Goddard et al. name trust and confidence among the drivers of over-reliance on automated advice (per the Skeptic's retrieval). M2 checks the reports. Nothing checks the reader.

**Determination: not labelled WEAKENING.** The residual is named in the change log without the label: *the seat formalises an intermediary between the seats and the CEO, and its limits are what stop that from becoming a filter.*

**What would overturn this:** evidence, from M1, M2 or M6, that the seat's reports omit or soften blocks or dissent. If that happened, the formalisation will have weakened 5.3 and 5.6 in practice, and the honest response is an amendment, not a relabelling. **Without Edit 3** the determination still holds, but the residual becomes larger and should say so.
