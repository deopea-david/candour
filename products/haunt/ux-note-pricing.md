## 0. The answers, before the reasoning

1. **The finding does not survive. I withdraw it.** *"Free + subscription cannot be built without a dark pattern"* rested on a dilemma — lapse either locks the journal or changes nothing — and the dilemma was false. A third branch exists, the CEO has settled it, and it is bound by Conditions 8.1–8.6. Tested pattern by pattern against Article 4's named list, **the shape deploys none of them.** This is a clean pass and I report it as one. §1.

2. **The block is not the finding, and the block stands.** B2 was a *release* block on *flows*. Its stated grounds have fallen; its subject has not. **It engages on implementations, not on the shape** — and §1.5 names the four implementations that would trip it. A seat whose reasoning is corrected and whose block survives should say both sentences, not just the comfortable one.

3. **Removing the free tier removes the thing a lapse lapses into, and nobody has noticed.** Condition 8.2's clarification reads *"A lapsed subscription returns the user to **the free tier's published window**."* The CFO recommends **no permanent free tier**. On that recommendation the clause has no referent, and the post-lapse state is undefined in every artifact in this repository. **This is the single largest open item in the pricing work and it is a product-shape question for the CEO.** §1.7.

4. **The trial should be designed so that nothing is taken away at day 14 except the engine.** Everything written during the trial stays readable, editable, searchable and exportable for ever; automatic capture stops; the venue page keeps telling the truth. That is the only expiry I can write honestly, it costs nothing to build, and it is the same answer as the CVO's fourth lapse branch. §2.

5. **The expiry moment's real hazard is not the paywall — it is the four things that sit around it.** The pending confirm queue, the `Always` location grant, the session that straddles the boundary, and the venue page's aggregates. Each is a route by which a compliant shape produces a non-compliant product, and none is in any requirements document. §2.5, §5.

6. **A trial that charges by silence and a product that may not send reminders are a bad pairing.** Apple's own page says the customer must *"cancel it at least 24 hours before the trial ends"* [E]; Haunt has no server, no account and no email address, so Candour cannot remind anyone. **Where the store offers a trial that does not auto-charge, take it.** Where it does not, the charge date must be a permanent, static, in-app status line — which Condition 8.4 permits and, on the C1-O11 clarification, requires. §2.4.

7. **The ladder is presentable honestly, and one tier is not presentable the way it is currently named.** Per-unit comparison, savings arithmetic and default selection are all fixable by rule. **"Lifetime" is not**, against a published five-year support commitment, and the paid-once tier's honest pitch is *"nothing to cancel"* rather than *"save money"* — because over five years it saves **exactly nothing**. §3.3, §3.1.

8. **The scheduled 32% cut is good news that becomes a dishonesty if it is discovered.** Apple applies decreases to existing subscribers automatically and offers no way to preserve the old price [E, CFO], so subscribers are protected by the store. **The person who can be surprised is the paid-once buyer**, who pre-pays five years on a date when Candour already knows the price falls. §3.4.

9. **Twelve would-be release blocks, restated for the PM/BA.** B1–B7 carried forward as they now stand (B2 amended, B3–B5 re-grounded on the new accessibility baseline), plus **B8–B12** covering the trial, the lapse, the ladder and the queue. Condition 4 of the decision record, as amended by C2.7, requires exactly this. §4.

10. **A correction I owe on my own note, found by re-retrieving rather than recalling.** The App Store sentence I cited three times as **5.1.1(ii)** is, on today's retrieval, **5.1.2(i)**. The finding is unchanged and the citation was wrong. §9.

## 1. The re-test

### 1.1 What the finding was, and what it rested on

`ux-note.md` §6.3, 2026-09-10, verbatim:

> *"**Cannot be built without a dark pattern. This is a finding, not a preference.** The test is a single question: **what happens when the user stops paying?** — If a lapse locks the journal, then the subscription is the history-window cap of §6.2 with a clock attached… Article 4 and Article 1.2. — If a lapse does not lock the journal, there is nothing the renewal buys except future updates… **There is no third branch.**"*

Two grounds and one structure. The grounds were **Article 4's export clause** and **Article 1.2**. The structure was a dilemma with two branches.

**Both grounds have fallen, and I do not contest either disposal.**

- **C1.1** on the export clause: *"Its modifiers — 'available at any time, at no charge and with no penalty' — attach to **export**, in a clause opening 'Let customers leave with their data'… They were never a requirement that export be experientially equivalent to the product."* The Constitution now says so on its own face, which is the part that settles it for me rather than a correction saying it: **Article 4, v1.2** — *"This clause governs exit — that the export is complete, free, immediate and without consequence. It is **not** a requirement that an export reproduce the product's own experience, and **it says nothing about what a product may or may not gate inside itself**."* My §6.3 used it to say something about what a product may gate inside itself. On the clause as it now reads, that use is not available.
- **C2.2** on Article 1.2: *"**No exploitation.** We do not profit from confusion, lock-in, addiction, or information asymmetry"* — and the disposal, *"Article 1.2 forbids profiting from a user's inability to leave; it does not forbid profiting from a user's preference to stay."* I have checked all four limbs myself rather than accepting the summary, because it is my finding that falls with them: **confusion** is answered by 8.1 and 8.6, **addiction** by 8.4, **lock-in** by 8.3, and **information asymmetry** by 8.5 and 8.6 plus the architectural fact that Candour never holds the journal. I find no limb the shape engages.

**C2.6 records the consequence and I adopt it without complaint:** *"Currently grounded on a surviving clause: zero… This is zero-now, not zero-forever, and it is not a criticism of the UX seat."*

### 1.2 The third branch, which now exists

The dilemma's force was entirely in *"there is no third branch."* There is one, and the CEO settled it inside Condition 8.2 on 2026-09-16:

> *"A lapsed subscription returns the user to the free tier's published window, and entries outside it become hidden — **not deleted.** They remain on the device, exportable in full at any time under 8.3, enumerable under 8.5, and restored in full on renewal."*

And the CVO had already identified a fourth, which §2 and §1.7 below recommend over the third: *"a lapse stops **automatic capture** while everything already written stays readable, editable and exportable forever"* [E, `proposals/haunt/ceo-product-inputs.md` §3, read from disk 2026-09-18].

**Why my dilemma missed them.** I wrote *"if a lapse does not lock the journal, there is nothing the renewal buys except future updates."* That sentence is the error, and it is a product error rather than a constitutional one: I treated *access to past entries* as the only thing a subscription could sell, and therefore concluded that any non-locking lapse sells nothing. **Automatic background capture on two platforms, a maintained venue index, a derived-session algorithm that improves for the life of the product, and a published multi-year support commitment are things a subscription can sell.** They are also, on the retrieved store rules, closer to what Apple requires a subscription to be: *"If you offer an auto-renewable subscription, you must provide ongoing value to the customer"* [E, verbatim — [App Store Review Guidelines 3.1.2(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), retrieved 2026-09-18]. A subscription whose only ongoing value is *not hiding things you already wrote* is a weak answer to that sentence as well as to mine.

The shape deploys none of Article 4's five named patterns. A disclosed-before-first-entry, never-retroactive, silently-passing, fully-exportable, fully-enumerable, restored-on-renewal lapse is buildable, and I can describe what it looks like — §2 does.

My charter, as amended: *"You fail by missing real problems, and you fail equally by manufacturing objections where none exist… A clean pass is a valid, reportable finding when it is the honest one."* This is that. I would rather record a withdrawal in one paragraph than spend three defending a sentence that two corrections and a constitutional amendment have overtaken.

**One thing I will not do, because it would be the same error in the other direction.** C2.3 records a harm my finding was reaching for and that no clause reaches: *"Candour would be **withholding**, in its own software, content the user authored and Candour never held. **That is a real harm and it is not lock-in.**"* I still think a read-cap is the wrong product for Haunt. **That is judgment, it is mine, it has now been tested against the Constitution's text twice and found unsupported both times, and it is recorded here as judgment and not as a block.** If Candour wants it binding the route is unchanged and is named in the decision record: a public Article 11 amendment, labelled as a strengthening.

### 1.5 What survives: the block, and the four things that trip it

**B2 is not withdrawn. Its reasoning is.** The distinction matters and C2.6 already drew it: *"That block is a *release* block on *flows*, and no flow exists."* A block whose grounds were wrong and whose subject was right is corrected by restating the grounds, not by deleting the block. B2's restated form is at §4.

**The four implementations that trip it.** These are checklist items, not unease, and each names what lifts it:

1. **Friction on the keep-gesture.** A keep action buried in an overflow menu, offered one entry at a time, with no bulk action, satisfies 8.5 on its face and is *"deliberately buried settings"* in function. The CFO has already published the arithmetic showing why this is the route commercial pressure takes first [E, `window-conversion-model.md` §2.1, read from disk 2026-09-18] and correctly recorded it as a flag with the block sitting here. **Lifts:** keep is reachable in one action from any surface an entry appears on, has a day-level and venue-level bulk form matching the confirm queue's bulk actions (`ux-note.md` §1.3), and is never styled as the secondary choice.
2. **Export implemented against the visible set.** 8.3 names this as *"the breach this article actually names."* It is also, per C2.8, the single change that revives Article 1.2 and with it the whole of my original B2. **Lifts:** QA exports from an account with hidden entries and diffs the export against the complete local store, as a release-gating test.
3. **Narrowing what counts as "visible."** The CFO found the open half of this and is right that nothing binds narrowness *at* launch, only tightening after it [E, `window-conversion-model.md` §2.2]. **Lifts:** the requirements document defines "visible" **once, exhaustively and affirmatively** — search results, venue pages, map pins, session summaries, timeline rows, aggregates, exports — rather than by exclusion, so 8.2's ratchet locks a definition written in the open. This costs a paragraph and it is the PM/BA's.
4. **Compulsion machinery at the moment of lapse.** 8.4's *"the window passes silently"* is easy to satisfy on an ordinary Tuesday and hard to satisfy on the day the subscription ends, which is when a product most wants to say something. §2.4 and B8.

### 1.7 The gap the re-test opened: no free tier, no window to lapse into

This is the most consequential thing in this note and it is not a criticism of any seat — it is what happens when two good decisions are made in different documents.

**If there is no free tier, there is no published window, and the clause that settles the lapse has no referent.** Three candidate post-lapse states follow, and they are not equally clean:

| Post-lapse state | Article 4 verdict | My position |
|---|---|---|

## 2. The trial, designed

The CEO's requirement is that people can try before buying. The CFO recommends a two-week full-feature trial and no permanent free tier. The CGO has already recorded the structure as clean in principle: *"a **time-limited full-feature trial** is neither a dark pattern nor a read-cap, and remains available"* [E, `products/haunt/compliance-note.md` §4.4, read from disk 2026-09-18 — note that this sentence sits **inside** a section carrying two correction banners; the trial sentence is not among the corrected reasoning, and I am citing it on its own terms].

**"Clean in principle" is where a design brief starts, not where it ends.** A trial is a promise with a date on it, and the date is the whole of the Article 4 surface. Everything below is about that date.

### 2.1 Two store mechanisms, and they are not equally honest

| | **Route A — auto-renewable subscription with a free-trial introductory offer** | **Route B — free time-based trial, then a full unlock** |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Apple's introductory offers: *"free trial, pay up front, and pay as you go"*; for a 1-month subscription the free-trial durations are *"3 Days. 1 or 2 Weeks. 1, 2, 3, or 6 Months. 1 Year"* [E, verbatim — [App Store Connect Help, *Set up introductory offers for auto-renewable subscriptions*](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-introductory-offers-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions), retrieved 2026-09-18] | Apple 3.1.1: *"Non-subscription apps may offer a free time-based trial period before presenting a full unlock option by setting up a Non-Consumable IAP item at Price Tier 0 that follows the naming convention: 'XX-day Trial.'"* [E, verbatim — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), retrieved 2026-09-18] |
| What happens on day 14 | *"After the introductory offer period expires, the subscription automatically transitions to the standard subscription pricing"* [E, same App Store Connect page]. **The user is charged by inaction.** Apple tells consumers: *"If you signed up for a trial subscription and you don't want to renew it, cancel it at least 24 hours before the trial ends"* [E, verbatim — [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/118428), retrieved 2026-09-18] | **Nothing.** The trial ends; no money moves; the user buys or does not. |
| Repeat-trial abuse | Apple handles it: *"each person is only eligible to redeem one introductory offer per subscription group"* [E, same App Store Connect page] | Candour's problem, and §2.6 is about why it should stay unsolved |
| Disclosure duty | Apple 3.1.2(c): *"Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price"* [E] | Apple 3.1.1: *"Prior to the start of the trial, your app must clearly identify its duration, the content or services that will no longer be accessible when the trial ends, and any downstream charges the user would need to pay for full functionality"* [E, verbatim] |
| DMCCA exposure | Engaged. A renewal cooling-off period triggers *"after a free or discounted trial ends"*, with reminder notices required on a durable medium [E, travelling from `pricing-ladder-model.md` §9.2 — **secondary, law-firm briefing, single underlying origin, not retrieved at source by me either**] | Not engaged: nothing auto-renews |

**Note the convergence, because it is worth seeing.** Apple's own trial-disclosure rule at 3.1.1 — *duration, what stops being accessible, and any downstream charge, stated before the trial starts* — is Condition **8.1** in different words: *"The limit, the window, and what happens at its end are stated in plain language… before the user writes anything."* Two rule-sets written by unrelated authors for unrelated reasons have landed on the same sentence. **That is usually a sign the sentence is right,** and it means a design that satisfies 8.1 is most of the way to satisfying store review.

**My recommendation, as this seat's judgment [J] with the reasoning attached:**

> **Take Route B if the store allows it. Route A is acceptable and carries three costs Route B does not: a charge by silence, a reminder duty Candour's architecture cannot discharge, and a countdown problem (§2.4).**

**The obstruction, stated rather than assumed:** 3.1.1's trial mechanism is offered to *"Non-subscription apps"*, and a ladder containing auto-renewable subscriptions is not one. **Whether an app selling both auto-renewable subscriptions and a paid-once unlock may use the 3.1.1 trial is not determinable from the guideline text I retrieved**, and I am not going to guess at Apple's reading of its own rule. It is a **CTO / CGO** item, it is answerable cheaply in App Store Connect and in App Review's own pre-submission channels, and it should be answered **before** the trial is specified rather than after it is built. If the answer is no, Route A is the design and §2.4 is load-bearing rather than precautionary.

### 2.2 What happens at day 14 to everything the user wrote — the direct answer

> **The trial's end may take away the engine. It may not take away the journal.**

Concretely, as acceptance criteria rather than as a principle:

1. **Every entry written during the trial remains readable, editable, searchable and exportable, for ever, at no charge, in the unpaid state.** No hiding, no read-only, no watermark, no delay, no cap on how many. This is not generosity; on the CFO's own recommendation there is no free tier, so **there is no published window for anything to fall outside of** (§1.7), and a design that invents one at the trial's expiry would be inventing a rule the user was never told (8.1) and imposing it on entries already written (8.2).
2. **Manual entry keeps working.** A trialist who declines to pay has a complete, honest, manual place journal that keeps everything for ever. That is the product in the unpaid state, and it is a real product rather than a husk. It is also the App Store 5.1.2(i) path for a user who refuses location (§2.5).
3. **The venue page keeps telling the truth.** Condition **8.6**: *"Aggregate and derived views — venue pages, average ratings, visit counts, session summaries — compute over the **complete** set of the user's entries, or state plainly on their face that they are partial."* With nothing hidden, they are complete and say nothing. This is the cheapest version of 8.6 that exists, and it is another consequence of not hiding anything.
4. **Export is unchanged and unconditional.** Condition **8.3**. The unpaid state's export is byte-for-byte the paid state's export. QA tests this by diffing (§1.5, item 2).
5. **What stops: automatic background capture**, and the venue-index refreshes and support that ride on the published support commitment. That is what the fourteen days demonstrated and it is what the subscription sells.
6. **Nothing is deleted.** Not the entries, not the venue rows, not the ratings, not the user's merges and renames (`ux-note.md` §2.3), not the session overrides (`ceo-product-inputs.md` §2.2). A user who subscribes eighteen months later finds their own corrections intact.

**If the CEO chooses branch (b) instead** — a published window that applies in the unpaid state — then everything in §1.4 applies and the day-14 moment must satisfy all six of 8.1–8.6, with §1.5's four implementations as the live risks and §2.3's expiry screen amended to state the window in the same words as the store listing. **It is buildable. It is more expensive, it converts worse on the CFO's own arithmetic, and it buys nothing that branch (a) does not.** That is my recommendation and it is judgment, not a block.

### 2.3 The expiry moment, designed

Three states. The design principle throughout: **the user should be able to predict day 14 from day 1, and day 14 should contain no information they did not already have.** A moment that surprises is a moment that was designed to.

**State 1 — from first run to day 13. A status, not a counter.**

A single static line, present unchanged from the moment the trial starts, in two places: the home screen's header area and Settings.

> *Trial — automatic capture is on until 2 October. Everything you write is yours and stays after that; only the automatic part stops.*

Under Route A, one further sentence, and it is not optional:

> *On 2 October you'll be charged 99p for your first month unless you cancel. Cancel any time in Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions.*

Requirements: the wording does not change as the date approaches; it does not change colour; it does not move; it is not a badge; it is never pushed. It states a date, not a number of days remaining — **a date is a fact, a counter is a clock** (§2.4).

**State 2 — the first launch after expiry. One banner, two equal-weight actions, no modal.**

At the top of the confirm queue, in the ordinary in-context style used elsewhere in the app:

> *Automatic capture has stopped. Your 63 entries are all here, and you can still add places yourself.*
> **[See the plans]** **[Not now]**

Requirements, and each of these is a checklist item the PM/BA can write:

- **Not a modal, not a full-screen takeover, not an interstitial on launch.** A paywall the user must dismiss to reach their own journal is *"deliberately buried settings"* applied to the whole product.
- **"Not now" dismisses permanently.** It does not reappear on the next launch, or after seven days, or ever. The state remains discoverable in Settings, which is where a status belongs (`ux-note.md` §4.2).
- **Equal visual weight.** No colour-coded steering, no "Not now" in grey 11pt.
- **No count of what is lost, because nothing is lost.** Under branch (a) there is nothing to count. Under branch (b), *"N memories expiring"* is forbidden by name in Condition 8.4.
- **No red, no warning iconography, no "your trial has expired" framing.** The trial did not fail; it finished.

**Explicitly barred from this screen (Article 4's named patterns, applied):** *"Don't lose your memories"* and every variant (false urgency); *"Are you sure? You'll stop remembering the places you go"* (confirm-shaming — and it is worth saying that this exact sentence is the one a competent growth designer would write, which is why it is named here in advance); a pre-selected tier in the plans sheet (pre-ticked box); a journal rendered greyed-out or disabled-looking when it is fully functional (a dishonest affordance, Article 1.3); any countdown (8.4).

**State 3 — for ever after. A permanent, factual row in Settings.**

> **Automatic capture:** off — trial ended 2 October. *Plans.*

That is the whole re-engagement surface, and it is a place the user goes to look rather than a thing that comes to find them. It is the same pattern as the backup status row, and for the same reason: *"a recurring prompt is the shape §1 rules out and the status achieves the same thing without it"* (`ux-note.md` §4.2).

### 2.4 The countdown problem: Condition 8.4 against the duty to warn

**This is the one place where two rules Candour has adopted point in opposite directions, and it needs settling in writing before it is built.**

- **Condition 8.4:** *"No countdown UI, no expiry notifications, no streaks, no badges, no 'N memories expiring' prompts… The window passes silently."*
- **Route A takes money on a date.** Apple tells the consumer to *"cancel it at least 24 hours before the trial ends"* [E]. Article 1.3 requires *"Pricing, capability, and limitations are stated plainly."* Candour has no server, no account and no email address, so it **cannot** send a reminder on any medium at all — the CFO has already flagged that the DMCCA durable-medium duty may be unservable on this architecture [E, `pricing-ladder-model.md` §9.2].

**Read literally and together, those produce a product that silently charges people.** That is the worst available outcome and neither rule intends it.

**The reconciliation is already in the record**, in the C1-O11 clarification of 8.4: *"What 8.4 forbids is **interruption and urgency**: anything that reaches for the user's attention, or counts down… **Silence is not the goal; non-interruption is.**"*

**The test I propose the PM/BA write, because it is checkable rather than a matter of taste:**

> **A statement of when money will be taken is disclosure. A statement of what you will lose, delivered to move you, is urgency.**

Applied:

| | Permitted | Forbidden |
|---|---|---|
| **Form** | A static line stating the **date** and the **amount**, present unchanged from day 1, in-app, in Settings and in the header | A number of days remaining that decrements; a progress bar; a badge; a notification of any kind; a modal; anything that becomes more prominent as the date nears |
| **Content** | *"On 2 October you'll be charged 99p unless you cancel"* | *"3 days left"*; *"last chance"*; *"you'll lose access to 63 memories"*; anything phrased as loss |
| **Timing** | Identical on day 1 and day 13 | Anything that appears only near the end |

**I state plainly that this is my reading and not a settled rule**, per the evidence standard's *"a seat's heuristic is not an article"*. Condition 8.4 was written to govern a **visibility window**, and a trial-to-paid conversion is a different event with money in it; I read 8.4 as not reaching a charge-date disclosure, and I read the C1-O11 clarification as supporting that. **If anyone disputes it, the seat that settles the scope of a gate condition is the CGO, not me** — and the dispute is worth ten minutes now rather than a release-gate argument later.

**And the cleanest resolution remains the one in §2.1:** under Route B nothing is charged, so there is no duty to warn, no countdown question, no DMCCA renewal cooling-off, and no reminder Candour cannot send. **The cheapest way never to ship a dark pattern around a charge date is not to have a charge date.**

### 2.5 The permission consequence at expiry — the one I would most expect to be got wrong

During the trial the user granted `Always` location. On day 14, under Route A or B, capture stops. **Three things must happen and one must not.**

**Must happen:**

1. **Location monitoring actually stops.** Not "continues and is hidden". Article 4: *"Collect the minimum data necessary, state why, and **delete it when no longer needed**."* Apple, independently: *"Data Minimization: Apps should only request access to data relevant to the core functionality of the app and should only collect and use data that is required to accomplish the relevant task"* [E, verbatim — App Store Review Guidelines 5.1.1(iii), retrieved 2026-09-18].
2. **The app says so, plainly, in the same status row as §2.3 State 3**, and offers the route to revoke the grant: *"Haunt has stopped using your location. You can remove the permission in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Haunt."* Telling a user how to take a permission back is the *"cancellation as easy as signup"* principle applied to consent rather than to money.
3. **The Privacy screen (`ux-note.md` §6.5) shows the live state**, as it does for backup and crash reporting. A claim the user can check beats a claim the user must trust.

**Must not happen — and this is a would-be block, B9:**

> **Capturing visits after expiry and offering them back on payment.** It is the most natural thing in the world to build ("we kept them for you!") and it is three named patterns at once: manufactured loss (*false urgency*), a pile of withheld things that grows while you do not pay (*engagement mechanics designed to exploit compulsion*), and data collected beyond the minimum necessary for a function the user no longer has (Article 4's data clause, and Apple 5.1.1(iii)).

**And the inverse trap, carried forward from `ux-note.md` §6.2 with its citation corrected.** An unpaid state must **never** request `Always` location for a feature it cannot use: *"Your app may not require users to enable system functionalities (e.g. push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality, content, use the app, or receive monetary or other compensation"* [E, verbatim — **App Store Review Guidelines 5.1.2(i)**, retrieved 2026-09-18]. **My three prior citations of this sentence gave the number as 5.1.1(ii); on today's retrieval it is 5.1.2(i).** See §9. The finding is unchanged: a user who declines location must still be able to use Haunt, so **manual entry is not a fallback mode — it is required**, and the honest consequence is that upgrading from an unpaid state means a fresh permission grant mid-purchase. That is a worse funnel and an honest one.

### 2.6 Anti-abuse, and the trap inside it

On a zero-network architecture a locally-tracked trial resets when the app is deleted and reinstalled. Somebody will notice this during build and propose a fix. **The fix is the breach.**

The available fixes are a device identifier persisted in the Keychain across uninstalls, or `identifierForVendor` retention, or any other scheme that makes the device recognisable to Candour after the user has removed the app. **On a product whose entire proposition is that it collects nothing, building a covert persistent identifier to enforce a trial is the largest single reputational and Article 4 exposure in this whole pricing exercise** — it is collection beyond the minimum necessary (Article 4), it is 5.1.1(iii) data minimization the wrong way round, and it converts the marketing claim in B7 from *nearly true* to *false*.

**My recommendation: accept the leak, and do not build the counter-measure.** Three reasons, and the first is not mine:

1. **Under Route A the problem largely does not exist**, because Apple enforces it per Apple ID: *"each person is only eligible to redeem one introductory offer per subscription group"* [E]. Candour should not rebuild, worse and covertly, an eligibility check the platform already performs.
2. **The population that will delete and reinstall an app to re-run a 14-day trial is small, and it overlaps almost entirely with the population that was never going to pay** [J — my judgment, no measurement, and it is not measurable on this architecture].
3. **The cost of being wrong is asymmetric.** Being wrong about (2) costs a few unconverted trials. Being wrong about building a covert identifier costs the product's only claim.

**Requirement for the PM/BA:** the trial's state is stored in the ordinary app container and is lost on uninstall, by design and on the record, so that nobody re-opens this under pressure in eighteen months without finding this paragraph.

## 3. The ladder — Article 4 audit of the tier presentation

The ladder audited is the CFO's proposed curve: **monthly £0.99 · quarterly £2.79 · yearly £10.90 · paid once £59.40** [E, `pricing-ladder-model.md` §3.2, read from disk 2026-09-18]. I audit **presentation**, which is my seat. The price *level* is the CFO's and the CEO's under Article 2.1 and Constitution 5.4, and nothing below is an opinion about the numbers.

### 3.1 The per-unit table, and the fact it exposes

Article 4: *"Use plain-language pricing with no hidden fees, forced bundles, or drip pricing."* The mechanism that satisfies it on a ladder is a single view in which every tier's per-unit cost is visible without arithmetic, scrolling or tapping:

| Tier | Charged | Per month | Per year | Saving vs monthly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | £0.99 each month | **£0.99** | £11.88 | — |
| Quarterly | £2.79 every 3 months | **£0.93** | £11.16 | £0.72 a year — **6.1%** |
| Yearly | £10.90 each year | **£0.91** | £10.90 | £0.98 a year — **8.2%** |
| Pay once | £59.40 once | **£0.99** over the supported life | £11.88 equivalent | **£0.00 — none** |

*(Arithmetic, re-derivable: 2.79 ÷ 3 = 0.93; 10.90 ÷ 12 = 0.9083; 59.40 ÷ 60 = 0.99; 59.40 against sixty monthly payments of 0.99 = 59.40. Against a five-year published supported life, paying once and paying monthly cost **identically**, which is the CFO's §8.2 finding — *"Identical. Not approximately — exactly, at every subscriber count"* — arriving at the pricing screen.)*

> **The finding the table produces: over five years the paid-once tier saves nothing at all. Its honest pitch is "nothing to cancel", not "best value".**

Any badge, ordering or copy presenting pay-once as the saving is a claim Candour cannot substantiate — Article 4, *"Remain honest in marketing: claims we cannot substantiate are claims we do not make."* What it genuinely offers is: nothing to remember, nothing to cancel, no renewal to be surprised by, and continued use after the supported life ends. **Those are real, they are worth saying, and none of them is a discount.**

### 3.2 The five checks, and the verdict on each

| Article 4 requirement | Verdict | The rule that satisfies it |
|---|---|---|
| **No pre-ticked defaults** — *"no pre-ticked boxes"* | **Satisfiable; must be specified** | The plans sheet opens with **no tier selected**. Purchase requires an explicit tap on a tier and then an explicit tap on buy. No tier is pre-highlighted, pre-expanded, or positioned as the resting state. |
| **Badges and steering** | **Conditionally permitted** | A badge is not a pre-tick, but it is a steer, so it must be **arithmetic rather than adjective**: *"Cheapest per month"* on the yearly tier is true and checkable against the table above. *"Best value"*, *"Most popular"* (unmeasurable — there is no telemetry, so Candour cannot know) and *"Recommended"* are not. **A claim about other customers' behaviour is unavailable to this product by construction**, and asserting one would breach the marketing clause. |
| **No confirm-shaming on cancel** — *"no confirm-shaming"*; *"Make cancellation as easy as signup"* | **Satisfiable, and the architecture helps** | The in-app cancel control is a **direct link to Apple's flow** — *"In Settings, tap your name. Tap Subscriptions. Tap the subscription. Tap Cancel Subscription"* [E]. **Nothing may be interposed:** no "before you go" sheet, no retention offer, no discount interstitial, no survey, no "are you sure". Worth recording that Candour **cannot** build a save-flow even under pressure, because with no server it does not learn a cancellation happened until the entitlement lapses. **The architecture forecloses the dark pattern.** That is a genuine benefit of the zero-network design and it should be claimed. |
| **No obscured per-unit comparison** | **Satisfiable; must be specified** | Every tier shows amount charged, billing period, and per-month equivalent **to the penny, in the same type size, in one view**. No "from 91p a month" headline anywhere — that is the ladder's version of drip pricing, because 91p is unavailable to anyone paying monthly and the monthly tier is what a user gets by doing the obvious thing. **If a single headline price is used, it is £0.99.** |
| **Honest savings claims** | **Satisfiable; must be specified** | Savings are computed **only against the monthly tier**, because that is the only alternative actually on sale; stated as both a percentage and the cash per year; **rounded down, never up**; and never against a notional or withdrawn price. 6.1% and 8.2%, not "up to 10%" and not "save 2 months". |

**One more, which is not on the list and belongs on it.** The pricing screen must state the **crossover** in plain words, because it is the one fact that decides the choice and neither store will tell the customer. The CFO reached this independently and drafted the same requirement from the cost side [E, `window-conversion-model.md` §4.5: *"the pricing screen states the crossover tenure in plain words"*]. On the five-year life the sentence is unusually clean and it should be printed:

> *"Over five years, paying monthly and paying once cost exactly the same: £59.40. Paying once means nothing to cancel, and it keeps working after [date]. We've committed to supporting Haunt until [date]."*

**Two seats reached this from opposite ends and it is one requirement, not two** — which, per the universal clause, is one concern and not two independent blocks.

### 3.3 "Lifetime" — the one item in the ladder I would block on

**Would-be block B11.** The word "lifetime", or "forever", or "for life", applied to the paid-once tier, against a **published five-year support commitment**, is two public numbers disagreeing.

- **Article 4:** *"Remain honest in marketing: claims we cannot substantiate are claims we do not make."*
- **Article 1.3:** *"**Honest by default.** Pricing, capability, and limitations are stated plainly. We say what a product cannot do."*

This is within my blocking scope, it is a checklist item rather than a feeling, and I flagged it before the ladder existed — `ux-note.md` §6.1, condition 1: *"Do not use the word 'lifetime.' It invites the reading Candour cannot honour."* The CFO has since reached the same conclusion from the cost side [E, `pricing-ladder-model.md` §8.4]. **Same concern, two seats, one block** — the independence test in `pipeline/evidence-standard.md` applies to seats as it does to sources, and I am not counting this twice.

**What lifts it:** the tier is named for what it is — **"Pay once"** — and the published support date appears on the same screen as the price, in body text, not in a footnote. The CFO's draft sentence is fine and I would use it: *"Pay once — £59.40. Supported until [date], the same as every other tier."*

**What does *not* lift it:** a footnote, an asterisk, a terms link, or "lifetime of the product" as a phrase, which is a distinction a reader will not draw and which the App Store listing will truncate.

### 3.4 The scheduled 32% cut — good news, and the two people who can be surprised by it

The facts, travelling from the CFO and not re-retrieved by me: a **32% price cut falls due on a calculable date at the amortisation cliff**, Candour can compute that date on launch day, and Apple applies decreases automatically — *"If you decrease the price of your auto-renewable subscription, existing subscriptions will automatically renew at the lower price. **You don't have the option to preserve the higher price for existing subscribers**"* [E, retrieved by the CFO 2026-09-18, [App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions), read from `pricing-ladder-model.md` §2.4 this session; **I did not re-retrieve it**].

**From this seat: subscribers are fine and the store protects them.** There is no Article 4 surface in a price falling automatically for everyone. The surface is in **discovery versus announcement**, and there are exactly two people it can reach.

**(1) The subscriber who finds out by looking at their bank statement.** Not a harm — the direction is in their favour — but a company whose entire proposition is transparency should not let its best news arrive as a surprise. **Requirement:** the pricing screen and the published cost sheet state the cadence in plain words, at point of sale, in advance:

> *"We review this price every year and publish the numbers. When the build cost finishes being paid off in [month year], the price falls. If you're subscribed, your renewals drop automatically — the App Store doesn't give us the option to keep you on the old price."*

That sentence is true, it is checkable against the published cost sheet, and it is the kind of thing Article 3's transparency schedule exists to make possible. **Announce it in-app as a static status and in the release notes, before the date, never as a push notification** (B1).

**(2) The paid-once buyer, who is the one who can actually lose.** A subscriber's price falls automatically. **A paid-once buyer's does not, because they have no ongoing price to reduce** — and Article 2.3's waterfall makes *"Price reductions for existing customers"* the first call on the remainder, a clause that has nothing to bite on for them. Someone buying "pay once" in year four is pre-paying five years forward, at the pre-cut rate, on a date when Candour already knows the rate falls in year six.

**Requirement, and it is the honest half of selling both shapes:** the paid-once tier's screen states that paying once does not participate in future price reductions, alongside the step-down date. One sentence. It is an unusual thing to print and it is the reason the tier can be sold honestly next to a subscription.

**And one thing I flag rather than block, because the seat is the CEO's and the article is not mine.** The CFO named a gaming vector at `pricing-ladder-model.md` §6.4: a company facing an automatic, unpreservable cut in year four has *"exactly one lawful escape: push new customers onto lifetime."* **The surface on which that escape is executed is a pricing screen** — a badge, an ordering, a default, a "limited" framing. Everything in §3.2 is written to make it visible if it is ever attempted, and B12 makes it a release-gate check rather than a matter of trust. **The Article 9 amendment itself belongs to the CEO on the CGO's advice** (Constitution 11), and I do not hold it.

WCAG2ICT also warns that *"ICT with closed functionality does not allow the use of some assistive technologies"* [E, same source] — **which does not bite here**, because iOS exposes VoiceOver, Voice Control and Switch Control to third-party apps. It is not a licence to conform less; it is a translation layer, and it is informative rather than normative, so where it is silent the WCAG text governs.

## 5. The confirmation queue and session segmentation, specified together

`proposals/haunt/ceo-product-inputs.md` closes with a commission to this seat: *"**UX** — specify the confirmation queue and session segmentation together."* The CVO's reason is right and I adopt it: *"confirming one night out with four venues is a materially better interaction than confirming four separate visits, and it reduces the queue pressure that UX was designing defences against."*

### 5.1 The joint specification

**The unit of decision is the session, not the visit.** That is the whole of the integration, and it upgrades the bulk actions in `ux-note.md` §1.3 from day-level to something that matches how the evening actually happened.

| Requirement | Detail | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Sessions are derived and recomputable** | Visits are the atomic record; a session is a grouping computed over them. Never persisted as immutable rows | The CEO's input §2.1, which the CVO calls *"the single most consequential line in this document"*. I endorse it and add the UX consequence: **a segmentation improvement must be able to re-group history that already exists**, or every improvement makes the product worse for existing users |
| **The queue groups by session, with day as the fallback** | One row per session: the venue chain, start and end, dwell in plain words — *"Friday evening, about 4 hours, 3 places"*. Expandable to the individual visits. Where segmentation confidence is low, fall back to the day grouping already specified | `ux-note.md` §1.2: *"Put enough context in the row to make it recognition again"*. A night is a more recognisable unit than a coordinate cluster at 21:14 |
| **Confirm-all and discard-all at session level, equal weight** | Extends the day-level rule. **Bulk discard must never be styled as the destructive or regretful choice** | `ux-note.md` §1.3. A queue that makes "no" harder than "yes" is a compulsion mechanic with good manners |
| **Split and merge, from the queue and from the timeline** | *"Any heuristic will merge two nights or split one, and this is a **journal** — the user's account of their own evening outranks ours"* (CEO input §2.2). The override persists separately and survives recomputation | Article 1.3. The same principle as venue rename-in-place (`ux-note.md` §2.3) |
| **An override is visible as an override, and reversible** | *"You split this night. Undo?"* available from the session, permanently | A correction the user cannot see they made is a correction they cannot undo when the algorithm improves |
| **Low confidence is stated, not guessed** | *"We weren't sure where this night ended"* rather than a silent guess at a boundary | `ux-note.md` §1.2: *"Do not ask a question the data cannot support."* Article 1.3 |
| **The home inference is visible and editable, and can be none** | A Settings row listing the places Haunt treats as home, editable, removable, with a plain statement of why it derives them | CEO input §2.3, and Article 4's *"state why"* applied to a derived fact rather than a collected one. This is a location inference about where a person sleeps; it is the most sensitive derived value in the product |
| **Accessibility** | A session row exposes **one** accessible label covering the chain, times and duration; split, merge and confirm are labelled controls; **every session function is reachable from the list, never only from the map** | **B3**, **B14**, and `ux-note.md` §5.3. A session drawn as a path on a map is the exact shape SC 2.5.7 and 2.5.1 exist to catch |

### 5.2 Where the trial's expiry meets the queue — four collisions, and none is in any requirements document

**(a) The pending queue at the moment of expiry.**

At day 14 the queue holds candidates the user has not yet confirmed. **These are not entries — they are proposals the user never adopted**, which matters because Condition 8 permits gating *"future creation"* and confirming a candidate is a creation.

**So gating confirmation is permitted. I recommend strongly against it, and the reason is Article 4 rather than generosity.**

> **A queue of locked candidates is a pile of withheld things that the user watched accumulate and cannot have.** It is *"N memories expiring"* built out of nouns instead of a notification — false urgency expressed as furniture, and an engagement mechanic whose whole efficacy is the pile. Condition 8.4 forbids the notification; building the same pressure into the home screen satisfies its letter and defeats it.

**The specification:**

1. **Capture stops. The queue stops growing.** Nothing new arrives.
2. **Candidates already captured remain confirmable**, free, for as long as they live. Gate the engine, not the disposal of what the engine already produced.
3. **Candidates continue to expire on the schedule that already existed** — default 30 days, user-adjustable, stated in onboarding, silent (`ux-note.md` §1.3). **The expiry rule is not changed by the trial ending.** A rule that fires because a payment did not is a rule the user was never told about, which is Condition 8.1 failing in the only way it can fail after launch.
4. **Never a count.** Not at expiry, not before it, not in a badge (B1), not in the banner (B8).
5. **Do not clear the queue at expiry.** A commercially-triggered deletion of the user's data, disclosed nowhere, is worse than everything above.

**(b) The session that straddles the boundary — the one a build will get wrong.**

A night begins at 23:00 on day 14 and ends at 02:00 on day 15. Because sessions are **derived**, a session can contain visits from both sides of an entitlement boundary — the trial's end, a lapse date, or (under branch (b)) the edge of a visibility window.

> **Requirement: entitlement and visibility are evaluated at session granularity, never at visit granularity. A session that began inside an entitlement is captured, presented and counted whole.**

**The clause this rests on is Condition 8.6:** *"Aggregate and derived views — venue pages, average ratings, visit counts, **session summaries** — compute over the **complete** set of the user's entries, or state plainly on their face that they are partial."* A session summary computed over three of a night's five venues is a derived view lying about the user's life, and it is the quietest possible way to breach 8.6 while satisfying everything else. **8.6 names session summaries by name and the segmentation feature did not exist when it was written** — this is where the two meet.

**(c) The venue page, and a reading of 8.6 that has commercial consequences nobody has stated.**

The venue page is, on the CVO's assessment, *"probably the product, not a feature"* (CEO input §1). Under branch (a) it is complete and says nothing about partiality, which is the cheap case.

**Under branch (b), read 8.6 carefully, because it settles more than it looks:** *"**Ratings and notes attached to hidden sessions still count.** A venue page must never show '3 visits, average 3.8' when the true figures are 11 and 4.2."*

> **So even under a visibility window, the venue page's *numbers* are complete. Only the *list of individual sessions* is windowed.**

Two consequences:

1. **A requirement:** the venue page shows the complete visit count and complete average rating, and marks which individual sessions are outside the window — **on its face, in body text, not in a tooltip, not behind an info icon** (*"deliberately buried settings"*).
2. **A finding, which runs against the window's commercial purpose and should be said out loud:** if the aggregate the user cares about is already complete and honest, **the window withholds far less than anyone modelling it has assumed.** This is the same conclusion the CFO reached from the arithmetic — *"the compliant version of this mechanism and the commercial version of it are different mechanisms"* [E, `window-conversion-model.md` §0.2] — arriving from the design side, on a clause the Skeptic wrote for an unrelated reason. **Two seats, two routes, one conclusion**; recorded as corroboration of a judgment, not as two blocks.

**(d) The moment itself is the most tempting in the product's life.**

Fourteen days of automatic capture produces the largest queue a user will ever see, and it arrives on the exact day the product wants money. **Every growth instinct points at that pile.** B8 exists because of this paragraph, and it should be read as the reason for B8 rather than as commentary.

### 5.3 What the trial does well, and one thing it does badly

**Well, and it deserves crediting because it solves a problem my own 2026-09-10 recommendation could only work around.** `ux-note.md` §6.1 recorded that a paid-up-front product cannot let a buyer check whether the venue index knows their local pub, and the best available answer was *"saying the coverage number out loud"* in the store listing. **A fourteen-day trial answers the question directly, for the user's own actual pub, before any money moves.** That is a materially better answer and the CFO's §5.2 objection to my workaround — that a number in a listing is a poor substitute — is well taken.

**Badly, and this is a usability finding about the mechanism rather than about the price [I, from the product's own structure].** The venue page is where a rating stops being a number on an event and becomes a standing judgment on a place, and **it needs repeat visits to be anything at all.** In fourteen days a typical user visits their local once or twice. **So the trial demonstrates the capture engine well and under-demonstrates the thing the product is actually for.**

I am not the seat that sets the trial length — that is the CFO's arithmetic and the CEO's decision under 5.4 — and a longer trial costs real money on the CFO's §9.3 table. **What I ask for is cheap and is a design requirement either way:** the trial's onboarding and the expiry screen state plainly what the venue page becomes over months rather than days, in words, ideally with the product's own honest example. *"Right now this says one visit. Come back in a year and it will be the record of a place."* That is true, it is not urgency, and it is the only way a two-week window can speak for a feature that needs twelve months.

## 6. Disagreements, recorded once each

Per the universal charter clause. Each is stated once, here, and I proceed either way.

**6.1 With the CEO, on which lapse branch to build.** The CEO settled the **third** branch on 2026-09-16: a lapse returns the user to a published window and entries outside it are hidden but never deleted. **I have found that branch buildable and I withdraw the finding that said otherwise** (§1.4). **I nonetheless recommend the fourth branch** — capture stops, nothing is ever hidden (§1.7(a)) — on three grounds that are all judgment and none of which is a breach:

1. It has **no Article 4 surface at all**, so Conditions 8.1–8.6 have nothing to govern and six acceptance criteria leave the requirements document.
2. It is **cheaper to build**: no two-result-set aggregates, no partiality banners, no window definition to ratchet-lock, no second permission tier.
3. It **sells the engine rather than access to the user's own memories**, which is a better answer to Apple's *"you must provide ongoing value to the customer"* [E, guideline 3.1.2(a)] than a subscription whose value proposition is un-hiding.

**This is a recommendation and not a block**, and I want the distinction on the record because my last word on this subject was a block: the third branch does not breach Article 4, and a seat that cannot tell the difference between "I would not build this" and "this may not be built" is not doing its job. **Constitution 5.4 puts product shape with the CEO. I proceed on whichever he chooses**, and §2 and §5 specify both.

**6.2 With the CFO, on the trial's mechanism.** `pricing-ladder-model.md` §9.3 models the trial as an **Apple introductory offer** on the monthly tier — Route A — and prices it at 15–31% of a converted subscriber's lifetime contribution. **I recommend Route B where the store permits it** (§2.1), because it removes a charge-by-silence, a DMCCA renewal cooling-off, a reminder duty this architecture cannot discharge, and the countdown problem at §2.4.

**And I record the cost of my own recommendation, because it is real and it runs against me [J].** The CFO's trial-to-paid rates almost certainly assume auto-conversion. **A trial that does not auto-charge converts materially worse than one that does** — the user must make an affirmative decision rather than fail to make one — and I have no number for how much worse and will not invent one. **So this disagreement hands the CFO a question rather than an answer:** Route B is the cleaner design and it is probably the more expensive one, and **the size of that gap is the CFO's to size, not mine.** If it turns out to be large, Route A with §2.4's rules built strictly is a defensible design and I would not block it.

**6.3 With the decision record, on a clause that now has no referent.** Condition 8.2's clarification is written around *"the free tier's published window"* and the pricing work has since recommended no free tier. **This is a flag, not a disagreement with anyone's reasoning** — both decisions were right when made — but it needs resolving in the same publication as the record itself (due 2026-10-16), not discovered by the PM/BA. §1.7. **Owner: CEO, with the CGO on the record's integrity.**

**6.4 Carried forward unchanged from `ux-note.md` §8**, and not re-argued here: the three disagreements with the CTO (the "not listed" path being necessary and not sufficient; the standalone accessibility week; the periodic recovery-code reminder), and the finding that design cannot make private writing stick without a mechanic Article 4 forbids. **The fourth — with the CFO on the free tier's compliant basis — is spent**, because the CFO has since recommended dropping the free tier for reasons that are stronger than mine were and that do not depend on Article 4 at all.
