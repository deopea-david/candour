## 2. The product as decided, in one page

A private, on-device journal of the places you have been.

- **Platforms: iOS *and* Android at launch** (D1, CEO 2026-09-20). React Native with Expo, with **native capture layers in Swift and Kotlin** (CTO, `android-and-stack-note.md` §2.7).
- **Capture is automatic; the record is not.** The OS (or, on Android, a clustering layer Candour builds) proposes a visit; **nothing enters the timeline without the user confirming it** (`proposals/haunt/proposal.md`, "What we are explicitly NOT building").
- **Fully offline.** Venue names come from a bundled Overture-derived index. **No places API, no analytics, no telemetry SDK.** The only outbound traffic is an opt-in backup and platform crash reporting the user has already enabled at OS level.
- **The venue page is the product**, not a feature: every visit, your rating, your notes, and an aggregate verdict on a place (`proposals/haunt/ceo-product-inputs.md` §1).
- **Sessions, not days.** A night out is the unit, and sessions are **derived from visits and recomputable — never stored as immutable rows** (`ceo-product-inputs.md` §2.1).
- **Pricing: a pay-once tier that leads, plus a subscription ladder — monthly and yearly; quarterly dropped (D14, 2026-09-22) — with a two-week full-feature trial and no permanent free tier** (Condition 9 as amended; C3.0).
- **On lapse the product becomes read-only: automatic capture stops, manual entry continues, nothing is ever hidden** (Condition 9.7, CEO 2026-09-19).
- **Haunts publishes no guaranteed support date, and says so where a buyer will see it** (D4, and Constitution v1.3 Article 4).
- **The product is named "Haunts", plural** (**D9**, CEO 2026-09-21). *"Singular reads as a ghost following you; plural reads as the places you frequent"* — and for a location-history product, being invisibly followed is the association the product exists to refuse. **The repository slug, branch names, codename and every identifier stay `haunt` and are not changing**; this document has been updated in customer-facing copy and product-name references only, and verbatim quotations of artifacts written before D9 are left as they were written. **"Haunts" is a working name until three checks pass — PLAT-6.**

**The thing this product sells** is the capture engine and the maintained venue index — not access to the user's own words. Condition 9.7: *"What the subscription now sells is the capture engine, the maintained venue index and the published support commitment — not access to the user's own words."* **The third limb of that sentence is gone with D4**, so the subscription sells two things, and §10 is written accordingly.

## 3. Definitions written once, because a ratchet is waiting to lock them

`products/haunt/ux-note-pricing.md` §7 item 8 assigns this to me in terms: *"No definition of 'visible' exists for Haunt… Condition 8.2's ratchet will permanently lock whatever definition is written first. It has not been written, so the ratchet is currently waiting to lock a blank."* It is written here, affirmatively and exhaustively, before anything is built.

**DEF-1 — "Entry".** An entry is any user-authored or user-confirmed record: a **confirmed visit**, its **rating**, its **notes**, a **venue's user-set name**, a **user merge**, a **session override**, and a **home-location correction**. An unconfirmed capture candidate is **not** an entry — it is a proposal the user has not adopted (`ux-note-pricing.md` §5.2(a)).

**DEF-2 — "Visible".** An entry is *visible* when it appears, unfiltered and without qualification, in **all** of: the timeline; search results; the venue page's individual-visit list; the venue page's aggregates; session summaries; map pins where a map exists; the export; and any count or total the app displays. **This list is exhaustive and affirmative.** A surface added later is covered by it unless this document is amended in the open.

**In the MVP as decided, every entry is visible in every one of those places, in every entitlement state, for ever.** There is no window, no cap, and nothing hidden (Condition 9.7). DEF-2 exists so that if a read-cap is ever proposed, Condition 8.2's ratchet — *"the window is a published number, it may be lengthened, never shortened, and never tightened for users who already wrote under it… the ratchet binds the window's scope as well as its number"* — locks a definition written when nothing turned on it.

**DEF-3 — "Session".** A grouping of visits computed over the visit record. A session is **never a stored immutable row**. It is derived from (visits + user overrides + entitlement intervals + capture-gap records), and may be **memoised in a cache that is rebuildable from those four inputs and discarded on any algorithm change** (`subscription-sizing-note.md` §7.1).

**DEF-4 — "Venue".** A Candour-owned local row with a local `venue_id` that is never derived from any dataset identifier. The Overture GERS ID is an **attribute**, never a handle (`feasibility-note.md` §3; `venue-index-remediation.md` §11).

**DEF-5 — "Capture gap".** A recorded interval during which Haunts was not watching, for any reason: permission downgraded, foreground service killed, Doze-suspended past threshold, device off, or **capture not entitled**. A gap is an *observation*, stored durably, never derived after the fact (`android-and-stack-note.md` §1.6; `subscription-sizing-note.md` §3.2, §7.1).

**DEF-6 — "Entitlement interval".** A durable, time-stamped record of what the user was entitled to between two instants. Entitlement is **never a boolean**: the question the journal asks is *"was capture entitled at 23:40 on 14 March?"* (`subscription-sizing-note.md` §3.2).

**DEF-7 — The usage model.** The Engineer has asked three times, in three notes, for a usage model and recorded that no Candour artifact carries one (`venue-index-nameseach.md` §10.2 item 4). **I am supplying one so the venue-index bars have a denominator, and labelling it for what it is.**

> **[J, PM/BA]** A moderately social user records roughly **100 visits a year across roughly 40 distinct venues**, weighted towards a handful of locals, at least one of which is in a dense city centre.

**This is adopted from the Engineer's own assumption at `venue-index-remediation.md` §8.4, not independently derived.** Per the evidence standard's independence rule it is therefore **one source, not two**, and it must not be cited as corroborated. It is a working assumption whose only job is to make "95% single-identity" mean something a person can picture: at 95%, about two of forty venue pages are wrong in year one.

## 5. The confirm interaction — CONF

**This is the product's core interaction and its biggest usability risk** (`ux-note.md` §1). It is also where **D8** put three binding constraints from measurement, and where the twenty-row question has to be resolved rather than admired.

### 5.1 The resolution I am making as PM/BA, and the trade-off I am accepting

Two correct design goals collide. UX designed the confirm row *"for recognition at a glance"* and twenty rows of a dense high street is not a glance (`ux-note.md` §1.2). The Engineer measured that at a five-row list single-identity falls to 93.9% and the pre-registered bar fails, while at twenty reachable rows it passes (`venue-index-nameseach.md` §7.3). The Engineer flagged the conflict rather than resolving it and said so: *"it is the UX seat's and the PM/BA's to resolve."*

> **Resolution: a short list by default, with typing as the path to the long one. The requirement is that twenty rows are *reachable in one action*, not that twenty rows are *displayed*.**

This is the shape `venue-index-remediation.md` §9.4 sketched and the measurements at §7.3 support. **The trade-off I am accepting, stated so it is not discovered later:** a user who does not type sees five rows, and on the Engineer's §7.3 table that configuration alone scores 93.9% rather than 100%. The design therefore depends on the search field being obvious enough that a user reaches for it when the first five rows are wrong. **That dependency is a usability question nobody has tested** (`ux-note-pricing.md` §7 item 1), and it is the single thing I would spend the usability-testing money on first.

#### 7.7.2 P5 — the residual repairable on-device, which is where the CVO's reservation is actually answered

**Why this is load-bearing rather than nice-to-have, in the shortest form I can write it.** D8 moves the source of venue identity from the coordinate to the user. That is a real structural fix — the as-specified index held one identity for **2.0%** of dense-centre venues after forty visits, and the remediated design holds it for **100.0%** at every visit count (`venue-index-remediation.md` §6.3; `venue-index-nameseach.md` §7.2). But it does not make the residual detectable **by Candour**: there is no server, no telemetry and no backfill, so *"a wrong confirmation is exactly as invisible to Candour as a wrong coordinate"* — which is why the decision record's D8 was **corrected on 2026-09-21** at the CTO's written disagreement, striking the CVO's claim that D8's mitigations *"meet that reservation"* [E, `decisions/2026-09-16-haunt-gate.md` D8 as corrected, read from disk 2026-09-21].

**The asymmetry that does answer it, and it is the sentence this whole section exists to serve:**

> **A coordinate-made error is invisible to everyone. A user-made error is invisible to Candour but visible to the user** — because the user knows where they went. So *"repairable by Candour"* was never available on this architecture and is not the standard; *"repairable by the person who holds the data"* is available, and it is the standard this product can meet.

**That answer is not free, and this is the price of it.** It is why merge, rename, reattachment and merge-undo are MVP rather than backlog, and why cutting any of them re-engages the block (S-3).

**Why C = 3, and how the screen avoids misleading anyone — VPAGE-11, this seat's proposal [J, PM/BA].**

- **C is "how many visits at your usual rating a new place is assumed to have".** At C = 3 a venue's own mean and the user's baseline carry equal weight at three rated visits; at ten visits the venue's own mean carries about three-quarters.
- **Three because a personal journal is small.** On DEF-7's usage model (~100 visits across ~40 venues a year; an adopted assumption, one source), a typical venue has two or three visits a year. The large C that review sites use for thousands of raters would flatten almost every venue in a personal journal to the user's own mean and make the order meaningless; a C of 1 barely tempers a single five. **Three is also the threshold VPAGE-10 uses for "enough to say something"**, so one number governs both.
- **It reproduces the CEO's example.** With m = 3.8, one five-star visit scores (3 × 3.8 + 5) ÷ 4 = **4.10**, matching D19's "about 4.1". Ninety-nine fives and a four score (11.4 + 499) ÷ 103 = **4.96**. **D19 records 4.98, which no single C gives alongside 4.1** — 4.98 implies C ≈ 0.85, at which one five-star visit would score about 4.45. The ordering is unaffected. **Flagged for whoever keeps the decision record, as arithmetic, not as a disagreement.**
- **What would change it:** usability observation that people find the order surprising at C = 3 (§22 item 1). I would not make C a user setting in v1: a control over an averaging constant is one almost nobody will understand.

**How the screen avoids misleading anyone about why the order differs from the stars.** Four rules, all in VPAGE-11's criteria: **(1)** the stars and figure shown are always the true mean with the visit count beside them, so nothing displayed is adjusted; **(2)** the weighted score is never displayed, so there is no second number to mistake for the rating; **(3)** a plain-words line says why the order is what it is, in body text, whenever that order is active — not a tooltip or an info icon (**VPAGE-3**'s standard); **(4)** the plain "highest stars" order is one tap away at equal weight.

**The boundary that keeps that true, written as LOOK-4(e) and repeated here in words, because it is the line that gets crossed by accident.** **The moment any affordance in this product transmits, publishes, addresses another person, or reaches a Candour-operated endpoint, it is D5's door — and it opens only through a fresh proposal and a full gate, on evidence of unprompted demand, never on a growth argument.** The Research Analyst's warning stands and is the reason this paragraph exists: a sharing layer *"will be exercised for company reasons and then justified with user reasons"*. **Handing a file to the operating system is not that. A button that offers to do it for you, unasked, is the first step of it.**

**Branding on the generated image — decided by the CEO on 2026-09-22: a small logo, not an overlay watermark, which the user can hide. Specified at LOOK-5.** What follows is this seat's view as given on 2026-09-21, **kept because it is the record of a disagreement, stated once and not repeated.** The CEO took the other side with both of the conditions I asked for, which is a reasonable place for this to land.

> **My recommendation (2026-09-21; superseded 2026-09-22): no Candour or Haunts branding on the image.**

**First, what is *not* true, because the easy answer here is the wrong one.** A small credit line is **not** a dark pattern. It is not deceptive, urgent, compulsive or a pre-ticked box; Article 4's named list does not catch it, and Article 1.1 states how Candour judges a product rather than prohibiting attribution. **Thousands of honest products do it.** **A seat that reached for "the Constitution forbids it" would be doing the thing `pipeline/evidence-standard.md` was rewritten to catch** — a heuristic operating as an article. So this is a values judgment, and it is the CEO's.

**Second, the judgment itself.** The artifact is a picture of the user's own life, made from data Candour never sees, and it is the single artifact of this product most likely to be looked at by other people. **The product's whole claim is that it takes nothing from you.** A watermark takes a small thing — a corner of that picture, as an unpaid marketing placement the user did not agree to — in the one place the claim is most visible. **The gain is a referral channel Candour cannot even measure** (no telemetry, by design, for ever); the cost is a small contradiction in the only claim the product has. **[J, PM/BA.]**

**Third, the honest argument on the other side, which is real.** Reach is this product's binding constraint — the CFO's §12 negative finding — and Haunts has no advertising budget, no sharing layer and no growth mechanism. **A watermark is the cheapest acquisition channel available and it is not dishonest.** **I record it because it is the strongest case against my own recommendation**, and because it is also, precisely, the shape of argument D5's warning tells us to be suspicious of.

**If the CEO takes branding anyway**, two conditions I would ask for and would write as criteria: it is **removable in the app**, by a control, not by cropping; and it is a **credit, not a call to action** — a name, never *"Get Haunts"*, a URL, a QR code or an install prompt. **Both were taken on 2026-09-22 and are LOOK-5.**

Kept as the record of the flag.)* **One conflict with the decision record, flagged rather than resolved here.** D10 as recorded accepts the recap *"with **no share affordance**"* and says *"any share affordance on a recap is sharing, and goes through D5's route."* The CEO's 2026-09-22 decision permits a user-created image. **I read the two as compatible** — a save-as-image control is an export format, and D10's worry was a button that invites showing it to someone — and LOOK-4's *"Save as image"* label and save-to-files mechanism are written to stay inside D10's words. **But the record should say so rather than leave a reader to reconcile them.** §22 item 16.

- **UX design time on top**, not sized here, and it is the scarcer resource: this is the first screen of the product and the one place a coverage failure is most visible.
- **No running cost.** The index is on-device (**VEN-1**), so marginal running cost is **£0** and Constitution 1.5 is satisfied structurally.
- **QA:** one fixture, reusing CONF-5's.

**The honest argument against it, which I am putting next to my own recommendation.** ONB-3 converts index coverage from a thing a buyer discovers slowly into a **first-run event that can fail immediately**. **My recommendation, for the estimation decision: take it, at 2–4 days, and take it *with* ONB-2 or not at all.** Seeding gives a user a success in the first minute; the index proof, on its own, can hand them a failure. Together they are a demonstration followed by a use. Separately, ONB-3 is the riskier half. **If the estimate comes back materially above four days, drop ONB-3 and keep ONB-2 — ONB-2 is free and carries most of the value.**

#### 12.2.2 The one limb linking does not answer, stated plainly

**A `PHAsset` local identifier is not the user's data in any sense Article 4 recognises.** It is an opaque handle into another app's database, on one device, on one install. Article 4 requires export *"in a usable, machine-readable format"*, and **a reference is neither usable nor machine-readable to anyone but the phone that wrote it.** An export containing references and no pictures would be lossless with respect to the *store* and worthless to the *person* — which is the precise failure **DATA-4** exists to catch, and DATA-4 is on the promise line at §20.1 because C2.8 records that weakening it *"revives Article 1.2's lock-in limb immediately"*.

**So if photos are part of an entry, Article 4's export duty attaches to the photos, not to the pointers.** The CEO's guessed resolution — references by default, real files on export — is the right shape and I adopt it. **But it has a consequence that must be specified rather than discovered:** a resolving export **can be partial**. The photo was deleted; the library permission was revoked; the image is in iCloud and not downloaded; the phone is offline. **A partial export collides head-on with DATA-4, which is written as absolute.**

> **The resolution, and it makes DATA-4 stronger rather than weaker: an export always states, per entry, whether each linked photo resolved, and names every one that did not.** DATA-4's *"zero divergence from the complete local store"* is then still exactly true — the store holds references, and the export holds the references, every file it could resolve, **and an explicit manifest of what it could not**. **A broken link that is named is honest. A picture that is silently absent is the breach.**

#### 12.2.3 What linking newly creates, which my original objection did not name

1. **A photo-library permission — on a product whose entire claim is that it asks for almost nothing.** **This may be avoidable entirely.** Both platforms provide an out-of-process picker — `PHPickerViewController` on iOS and the Android Photo Picker — which returns a user-selected image **without** the app holding library authorization. **[K, PM/BA, not retrieved this session, and load-bearing — it must be upgraded to [E] by the Engineer or CTO before it anchors a design.]** **The open question it leaves is the crux and it is not mine:** whether a **durable, re-resolvable** reference is obtainable through the picker path on both platforms without full authorization. If it is, photos ship with **no new permission at all**. If it is not, the design degrades — §12.2.5.
2. **App Store 5.1.2(i) again.** Photo functionality must never be required to reach anything else — **PHOTO-2**, and it is the same rule as **CAP-6**, **CONF-16** and **ONB-1**.
3. **Other people's faces.** A photo of a night out contains third parties who never consented, it stays on-device, and Candour never sees it. **That is the identical posture to companions (CONF-19), and it is the same household-activity question the CGO already owes under D10(a)** — I am **joining it to that question rather than opening a second one**, because two versions of one ruling is how a gap becomes furniture.

#### 12.2.4 Verdict

**Linking answers two of my four limbs outright, materially reduces a third, and relocates the fourth onto DATA-4 — where, specified as above, it survives and leaves DATA-4 in better shape than it found it.** **I withdraw my recommendation against photos for v1.** I record that I was answered by a design rather than by insistence, which is the version of this exchange worth having.

**The five conditions the withdrawal rests on.** If any fails, this comes back to the CEO rather than being quietly built anyway.

1. **C-1 — the CTO confirms a durable, re-resolvable reference on both platforms, and says whether it needs library authorization.** *(Open question 15.)*
2. **C-2 — export resolves and includes the files, with an explicit unresolved manifest.** PHOTO-3.
3. **C-3 — backup carries references only, and the backup screen says so before the user relies on it.** PHOTO-4.
4. **C-4 — the thumbnail cache is a cache: bounded, evictable, rebuildable, and deleting it loses nothing.** PHOTO-5.
5. **C-5 — the size statement is a *statement*, never a gate.** PHOTO-6, and see the warning below.

**On C-5, a flag I want on the record because the suggestion as put to me is one step from a breach.** A *"size warning"* before export is a very short distance from **friction on the exit path**, which Article 4 forbids in terms — export is *"available at any time, at no charge, and **with no penalty or disadvantage imposed for having exported**"* — and which UX **B2**(iii) names directly. **A statement of the size is disclosure. A confirmation step the user can fail, a discouraging tone, or a default that omits photos to keep the file small, is a penalty.** The distinction is the same one UX drew for the charge-date at ENT-10 and I am applying it here.

#### 12.2.5 If C-1 fails

**The requirement degrades; it does not disappear.** If no durable reference is obtainable without full library authorization, the CTO's options are (i) photos require the permission, in which case **PHOTO-2** and ONB-1 govern and the whole feature must be skippable and absent by default; or (ii) copy-on-add with a bounded count and an explicit size statement — **which is my original objection back in full, at which point it returns to the CEO as a decision rather than being absorbed.** **I am not choosing between those here. The CTO owns the storage and export consequences and I am naming what I need rather than deciding it alone.**

**What I need from the CTO, in one list:** C-1's answer on both platforms; the thumbnail-cache design and its bound; the export path's peak memory and whether it streams (a multi-gigabyte zip must not be built in RAM on a phone); the marginal build hours; and whether any of this touches the **DATA-2** four-consumer SQLite contract. **What I need from the CSO:** nothing new, if linking holds — but **DATA-15**'s foreign-archive parsing and PHOTO-3's file handling are the two new untrusted-input surfaces in this document and that seat should see them together.

#### 12.2.7 The CEO's decision of 2026-09-22, and the missing photo as the normal case

**The decision, in the CEO's terms.** Store references to the user's photo library, never copies. The export includes the actual files where they are still available. Where a referenced photo is no longer available, the app and the export hide it or show a plain "no longer available" message, never a broken reference and never a silent failure. **The missing-photo state is a first-class, normal long-run case, not an error path.**

**My honest assessment of whether linking answers the objection or relocates it.** It does both, and in known places. **It answers storage, backup size and diagnostic redaction** (§12.2.1): the store grows by bytes, the backup stays small, and image bytes and their EXIF never enter the app's container. **It relocates the export problem**, from *every day* to *export day*, and onto **DATA-4**, which is on the promise line. **The include-files-on-export decision is what answers that relocation**, and with PHOTO-3's unresolved manifest it answers it honestly. **So on the four limbs as I originally wrote them, the CEO's design is a genuine answer, not a relocation dressed as one.**

**What the 2026-09-21 assessment did not say, and I am adding now: the problem also relocates in *time*.** A reference is only as durable as the user's library. People change phones, turn off cloud photos, clear space and delete pictures. **Over the multi-year life this product is built for, a large share of references will stop resolving — for many users, all of them at the first phone change without a synced library.** [J, PM/BA; no data on how fast references decay exists in this repository, and there is no telemetry to learn it (**PRIV-8**).] Three consequences follow, and they are why the CEO's "normal long-run case" framing is the right one rather than a nicety:

1. **Export completeness for photos decays.** The export carries the files that are still available **on the day it is made**. A photo deleted from the library in year two is not in a year-five export, and nothing Candour does can bring it back. **That is honest only if it is said at the moment the user attaches a photo**, not discovered at export — PHOTO-10. **Haunts is not a photo backup, and must not let a user believe it is.** The same applies to Article 7.2's post-shutdown export: it will carry only what is still there.
2. **The missing state is the state most photos are in eventually**, so it must be designed, measured and tested as a primary state — PHOTO-8.
3. **Moving to a new phone or the other platform breaks every reference by construction.** DATA-5's *"full fidelity"* is therefore read, for photos, as: every entry and every reference round-trips, and the **files travel inside the archive**; a reference from another device resolves as unavailable unless the user chooses to put the files back in their library — PHOTO-9.

*(Superseded 2026-09-22 by **D12** — see §12.2.8. #### 12.2.8 D12 — photos fetched from the user's own cloud, and the claim reworded

**What D12 decided.** Fetching a photo the user attached, from the user's own iCloud or Google Photos, to show it back to them, is **not a blocker**: the request runs from the user's device to the user's own storage through the platform framework, and **Candour receives nothing and sends nothing new**. What breaks is a **sentence**: PRIV-1's *"The app makes no other network calls"*, which CRA 2015 s.36(3) makes a contract term. **So the claim is reworded rather than the feature blocked.** No manual download button; the fetch is automatic. A display-size thumbnail is kept at attach so something always shows (**PHOTO-1**, **PHOTO-5**, **PHOTO-7**). **I agree with it.** The substance of the privacy claim was always *who receives your data*, and this changes nothing there. The old sentence claimed more than that, and a broader claim is not better if it stops being true.

**What it does to the thinning problem.** The thumbnail substantially answers it: a photo whose original has gone still shows. **What still thins is the export's originals** — a year-five export carries originals only for photos still in the library, and thumbnails for the rest. That is honest and is said at attach (**PHOTO-10**).

**The PRIV-1 wording — approved by the CEO at D14(c), 2026-09-22.** The **CGO** must still confirm it as a contract term before it ships:

> *"Your timeline, notes and ratings never leave your device unless you switch on backup. If you do, an encrypted copy goes to your own cloud — encrypted with your passphrase, which we never see. **Haunts sends your data nowhere else. If a photo you attached is kept in iCloud Photos or Google Photos, your phone may fetch it from there to show it to you.** If you opt in to the platform's crash reporting, the platform receives crash diagnostics — never your journal."*

**Why these words and not the D12 example.** D12's example — *"Haunts never sends your data anywhere"* — is false when backup is on, since backup sends an encrypted copy to the user's cloud. *"Nowhere else"*, after the backup sentence, is true. I have avoided *"nothing is uploaded"*: a fetch sends a request, and the claim should not depend on how a reader understands "upload".

**What D12 does not settle, and the wording must not pre-empt.** D12 draws the line itself: *"A map tile request sends the locations a user is viewing to a third-party tile server"* — and that is not covered by the CEO's argument. **If the CTO picks an online tile source for the heatmap's map, the reworded sentence becomes false again.** §18 records the referral.

## 15. Accessibility — A11Y

### 16.1 Condition 10 — the EU storefront disposition

**Condition 10:** *"EU storefront disposition (CGO): either restrict availability to the UK at launch, or accept the residual and rely on the European Accessibility Act microenterprise exemption. To be chosen at requirements, not discovered at submission."*

**This seat's recommendation: restrict availability to the UK at launch.** Four reasons, and the first is the only one that is about risk:

1. **The exemption is available but the boundary is not resolved.** The CGO found Article 4(5) exempts microenterprises *providing services*, and Article 3(23) defines one as fewer than 10 persons and turnover under EUR 2 million — Candour is one person — **but recorded plainly that *"the products/services boundary for an app is not something I can resolve"* [J, flagged]** (`compliance-note.md` §5.2). Relying on an exemption whose applicability a seat has flagged as unresolved is relying on an unresolved thing.
2. **The venue index is a UK index.** Overture extract, UK bounding box, FSA-validated in three UK areas. An EU storefront sells a product that does not know the user's local.
3. **The whole consumer-law analysis is UK.** DMCCA, CRA 2015, ICO, PECR. An EU launch adds a second regime nobody has analysed, and Condition 9.6 already puts one qualified human legal review on the critical path.
4. **It is the reversible choice.** Adding storefronts later costs a form. Withdrawing from them after selling is a discontinuation-shaped event.

**Against my own recommendation, recorded because it is real:** the CGO also wrote that *"Meeting WCAG 2.1 AA properly makes the question largely academic either way"*, and §15 of this document commits to the **higher** 2.2 AA baseline. So the accessibility limb of the risk is largely answered by work Haunts is doing anyway. **The recommendation therefore rests mainly on reasons 2 and 3, which are product and legal-scope reasons rather than accessibility ones.**

**This is prepared, not decided.** Constitution 5.4 reserves *"release to real users"* to the CEO, and storefront availability is a release decision. **PLAT-5 records whichever he chooses.**

### 19.3 Backlog — a readable `haunt.html` in the export

**Status: CEO decision, 2026-09-21 — not MVP, low priority. Recorded as backlog with its reasoning intact, at the CEO's instruction, so that a later reader gets the argument and not just the verdict.**

**What it would buy.** The export stops being a thing only a developer would open. A nicer export does not fill it, and must not be used as though it had.**

## 20. The promise line and the cut line

### 20.1 The promise line — what cannot be dropped without breaking the promise

The template asks which requirements, if dropped, break the promise made in the proposal. **Seven, and each names the promise it carries.**

1. **DATA-4 — export complete and unconditional in every state.** This is the one clause of Condition 8 that survives the removal of the read-cap, and C2.8 records that weakening it *"revives Article 1.2's lock-in limb immediately"*. It is also what makes "you can leave" true.
2. **VEN-7 … VEN-12 — the schema rules.** With no server, no telemetry and no backfill, a refresh that mutates a referenced venue row is *"a defect that cannot be detected after ship"*. These are free now and unavailable later.
3. **CONF-3 — never auto-select.** It is what converts a measured 57.5% wrong-first-row rate into a scroll rather than a corrupted journal, and it is Article 4's pre-ticked-box clause applied to the venue. **Drop it and the venue page — which the CEO calls the product — becomes silently, permanently wrong.**
4. **SESS-1 — sessions derived, never stored.** *"Every future improvement to the segmentation algorithm either corrupts existing history or cannot be applied to it."* It costs nothing now.
5. **PRIV-1 and PRIV-8 — the transmission claim and the enforcement that makes it true.** The single honest differentiator that survived discovery is *"literally no outbound calls"* — **narrowed by D12 (2026-09-22) to its substance: Candour receives nothing, and your data goes nowhere but your own cloud** (§12.2.8). A claim the app cannot substantiate is the one thing Article 4 forbids by name, and CRA 2015 s.36 makes store copy a term of the contract.
6. **CAP-4 — capture gaps as first-class data.** The word *"automatic"* is substantiable on Android **only** alongside this surface. **Ship without it and the CTO's disagreement stands**: we would be claiming something we know fails silently on part of the fleet.
7. **VEN-21 — the repair layer, in all four limbs.** *(Added 2026-09-21 with the Block 4 ruling.)* D8 makes the **user** the source of venue identity, and a user-made error is invisible to Candour. **The only answer the CVO's silent-corruption reservation has is that the person holding the data can see the error and repair it** (§7.7.2). Cut merge, rename, reattachment or merge-undo and that answer fails with it — which is why it is the CTO's standing condition **S-3** and re-engages Block 4 on limb (b). **Whoever proposes this cut meets the block, not a trade-off.**

### The three I would take first

**1. Prove the index knows their local, on the first screen, before any permission.** *(New. Not yet in the tables.)*
First run offers one thing: **"Find your local."** Type four letters; see it found; that is the whole of onboarding's first beat. It exercises prefix search (CONF-5) and the twenty-row path (CONF-4) at the exact moment the user is most willing to learn them; it answers the coverage-honesty problem UX raised — that a buyer cannot know whether the index knows their pub — **by demonstration rather than by a number in a store listing**; and it gives the trial a success on day one instead of on day three. **Cost: small, it is the existing search screen with different framing.** **Conflicts: none I can find.** It makes a claim checkable inside the app, which is the same move PRIV-2 makes for transmission.

**2. Let the first run seed the journal from memory.** *(New.)*
Immediately after suggestion 1, offer **"add a place you already know you've been"** — the manual composer (ENT-4), used on day one. Today the product is empty for as long as it takes to go out, and **the venue page — which the CEO calls the product — needs repeat visits to be anything at all**, which a fourteen-day trial cannot supply (`ux-note-pricing.md` §5.3, recorded as an open item nobody had answered). **Seeding lets a user build a venue page's worth of history in two minutes from things they already remember.** **Cost: none beyond ENT-4, which is already BLOCKING scope.** **Conflicts: none.** This is the single cheapest thing in this section and I think it is the most valuable.

**3. Put a readable journal in the export.** *(New.)*
Add `haunt.html` to the export zip: one self-contained file, no network, no dependencies, opens in any browser, containing every entry, every venue page and every session. **Cost: modest — one template over the same serialiser DATA-3 already builds.** **What it buys:** the export stops being a thing only a developer would open, Article 7.2's discontinuation promise becomes something a person can actually hold, and it is a genuinely uncommon thing for a paid app to ship. **The honest conflict, and it matters:** Constitution Article 4 says the export clause *"is **not** a requirement that an export reproduce the product's own experience"* (v1.3, Article 4). **That cuts both ways.** Making the export richer is a gift, not a discharge of an obligation — and it must **never** be offered as a reason to make the app poorer. If anyone ever argues "we can gate X in-app because the export is so good", that argument is barred by C1.1's reasoning and by this paragraph.

### Group A — the journalling loop

**A1. Ask who you were with.** A chip-style free-text field on the confirm row — **typed by the user, never read from the contacts book**. Companions are the thing people actually remember about a night out, it makes search dramatically better later, and it is one more text column. **Conflicts to weigh honestly:** it stores other people's names on the device. The household exemption position is unaffected because Candour never receives it (`compliance-note.md` §3.2), but Article 4's *"Collect the minimum data necessary, state why"* means it must be optional, explained in one line, and **must never touch the address book** — the moment it reads contacts it becomes a permission request and a different product. **Cost: small.** **My view: worth doing, with the contacts prohibition written as an acceptance criterion.**

**A2. A session recap worth re-reading.** The session view exists for confirmation (SESS-3). **Make it also a thing you would open a year later**: the chain of places, the walk between them, the times, your ratings and notes, on one page. This is the "raise the value of re-reading" family UX named as legitimate, and it is what makes segmentation feel like a feature rather than a grouping rule. **Cost: a screen.** **Conflicts: none — provided it is found on opening the app and never pushed (B1).**

**A3. Search that includes notes, offline, from day one.** Already implied by FTS5 being in the index; **say it out loud as scope** rather than letting it be discovered. UX listed it under raising the value of re-reading. **Cost: near zero given VEN-4.**

### Group B — the venue page

**B1. Be honest about small numbers.** *(Written in as **VPAGE-6**, MUST.)* Do not print "5.0" from one visit. **Cost: trivial. Conflicts: none.** Reject it and VPAGE-6 is deleted.

**B2. Offer merge where the product can see a probable duplicate.** *(Written in as **VEN-16**, MUST.)* The repair layer is useless if the user never notices the damage — and the Engineer's measurements say duplicates are the residual after everything else. Static, in-context, on the venue page, no notification, no count. **Cost: small. Conflicts: none, because Condition 8.4 as clarified expressly permits static in-context disclosure.**

**B3. Show the trend, not just the mean.** *"You've rated this 4, 5, 4 — going up."* A mean is the least interesting thing a venue page can say about eleven visits. **Cost: trivial.** **Conflicts: none.** **My view: this is what makes the page feel like a judgment on a place rather than a database view — which is the CEO's own framing of why it is the product.**

**B4. A local, honest self-portrait.** *"You rate pubs 4.1 and cafés 3.2. Your most-visited place is X. You've been going there for three years."* Computed on-device, from the user's own data, found on opening the app. **Cost: small.** **Conflicts — and this is the line to be careful about:** *"you've been coming here for three years"* is a **retrospective fact** and is legitimate; *"you're on a 6-week streak"* is a **compulsion mechanic** and is barred by Article 4 and B1. **The test: does it describe the past, or does it create a thing to maintain?** Anything in the second category is out.

### Group C — what an export is for

**C1. A manifest with checksums**, so a user can verify a five-year-old export is intact. **Cost: trivial.** Fits the "claims you can check" theme that runs through PRIV-2 and LIC-4.

**C2. Import from a competitor's export.** The documented trust injury in the research is Google Timeline destroying users' history, and migration is the only acquisition mechanism available to a product with no sharing layer and no ad budget. **I am flagging this rather than recommending it**, for three honest reasons: it is a parsing surface against a format Candour does not control; it is the only place a third party's data structure enters the product; and it would need its own venue-matching pass, which is the thing Block 4 is held over. **Cost: real, and it is scope.** **My view: attractive, and not for v1.**

### Group D — honesty surfaces that are also features

**D1. Make "when Haunts wasn't watching" browsable.** CAP-4 already records gaps and renders them inline. **Make them also a list**, so a user can answer "why is there nothing from March?" in one place. **Cost: a screen over data that already exists.** **Conflicts: none.** It also happens to be the surface that carries the Android OEM problem honestly — the CTO's point that failing *visibly* is an honest product, and failing *quietly* is the opposite of the promise.

**D2. "What Haunts has worked out about you."** SESS-6 already requires the home inference to be visible and editable. **Generalise it**: one screen listing every derived fact the product holds — homes, session boundaries, venue identities it guessed, merges it made automatically — each editable or removable. **Cost: a screen.** **Conflicts: none, and it is the natural extension of Article 4's "state why" from collected facts to derived ones**, which is what `ceo-product-inputs.md` §2.3 asked for on one fact and which costs almost nothing to do for all of them. **My view: this is the kind of thing a privacy community recommends unprompted, which is Article 1.1's own test.**

### What I considered and am recommending against

- **Anything that counts, nudges, celebrates or maintains.** Barred by Article 4 and by B1, and the bar is not negotiable at the design stage.
- **A widget or watch app at v1.** Both are capture surfaces that would need their own permission and entitlement story, and the build is already 2,090 hours.
- **Photos on entries.** Attractive and obvious — and it changes the storage profile, the export size, the backup size and the diagnostic-redaction problem all at once, and it is the single most common route by which a "small local file" becomes a gigabyte the user did not expect. **Not for v1. Worth a proper look at v2 with the CFO on storage and the CSO on the redaction test.**
- **Anything requiring a network.** The FSA refresh is the live case and §19.1 handles it.
