## What was decided and why

The CEO decided **PROCEED on the commercial branch**: a one-off purchase, iOS only, on the zero-network architecture — the only pricing shape the UX seat found buildable without a dark pattern.

**The CEO's stated reason, recorded in his own terms rather than paraphrased into a business case:**

> *"I quite like this idea for myself personally and if I can release it and it makes a bit of money then I am happy with that."*

**This record states that plainly because the alternative would be dishonest.** The pack does not contain a commercial case for Haunt, and this decision does not claim one. Break-even on benchmarked labour is **~2,400–2,700 sales over three years** against a category leader with 164 UK ratings after a decade; every seat that examined reach concluded it was the binding constraint. The CEO has decided to build something he wants to exist and to sell it honestly, accepting that it may not cover its own benchmarked labour. Under Constitution 5.4 that is his decision to make. Under Article 2.1 the labour is still costed at benchmark whether or not it is drawn, so the published cost sheet will show that gap rather than hide it.

**This decision went against the CVO's recommendation (KILL) and against the weight of the discovery pack.** That is recorded here without softening, as is the Skeptic's governing sentence:

> *"If the CEO's decision is anything other than KILL, this pack is not fit to support it."*

The CEO has not disputed that finding. The conditions below exist because of it: this is a **proceed to requirements on a pack known to be deficient in four named places**, not a proceed to build on a settled case.

**Why the product survived on the merits it did survive on.** Discovery replaced the sparked architecture with a fully offline one, which is the reason a PROCEED is coherent at all: it removed the venue-name licence problem (which the CGO found is *deferred, not avoided*, on any API architecture), took the per-user running cost to £0, and made the privacy claim substantiable rather than hedged. The CGO's finding is recorded verbatim in effect: **on this architecture Candour is neither controller nor processor of journal data, is party to no third-party data terms, and Article 7.2's open-sourcing promise is deliverable in full. The regulatory case against Haunt is nil.**

**Article 8 designation was considered and not taken.** The CFO asked that it be a live option; the CVO wrongly omitted it; it was restored at full strength in the gate pack and put to the CEO as one of four options. The CEO chose the commercial branch. Designation is therefore **not** foreclosed by this record — Article 8 designation remains available at any future gate, and is permanent only once taken.

A lapsed subscription returns the user to the free tier's published window, and entries outside it become hidden — **not deleted.** They remain on the device, exportable in full at any time under 8.3, enumerable under 8.5, and restored in full on renewal. What 8.2 forbids is Candour shortening the window afterwards or tightening terms on entries already written; it does not freeze a paying tier's entitlements permanently, which would leave a subscription with nothing to buy.

Raised by the Skeptic in the C1 review as the quietest and most effective way a visibility window could breach Article 4 while satisfying 8.1–8.5; adopted by the CEO 2026-09-16.)*

   **8.4 No compulsion machinery.** No countdown UI, no expiry notifications, no streaks, no badges, no "N memories expiring" prompts, no cap on how many entries may be marked. The window passes silently. *(Article 4 — no false urgency; no engagement mechanics designed to exploit compulsion.)*

   *Clarified 2026-09-17 (Skeptic, C1-O11).* What 8.4 forbids is **interruption and urgency**: anything that reaches for the user's attention, or counts down. It does **not** forbid **static, in-context disclosure** — an entry's own screen may state plainly that it sits outside the current window and how to keep it. As originally drafted, 8.4 made the cheapest build the worst one: say nothing at all, and let the user discover the loss by absence. **Silence is not the goal; non-interruption is.** 8.1 governs what is disclosed before the first entry; 8.5 and 8.6 govern what remains visible and honest afterwards.

# Correction C1 — 2026-09-16, before first publication

**Classification: CORRECTION, not WEAKENING.** The reasoning for that choice is recorded at C1.6, because the CGO recommended the WEAKENING label and the CEO and CVO decided against it. A reader is entitled to see the disagreement rather than the outcome alone.

**Condition 8 as adopted read:** *"no limit on reading, editing or exporting entries the user has already created — in any tier, at any price, ever."* It was supported by this sentence, which the record called the most valuable it had produced: *"UK consumer law permits a clearly-disclosed read-cap; Candour's own constitution forbids it."*

**The second half of that sentence is false.** The CEO challenged its basis; the CGO re-examined and conceded both grounds in full.

**C1.1 — Article 4's export clause was misread.** Its modifiers — *"available at any time, at no charge and with no penalty"* — attach to **export**, in a clause opening *"Let customers leave with their data"*, mirrored at Article 7.2 in the discontinuation rules. They require that exporting be free, immediate and without consequence. They were never a requirement that export be experientially equivalent to the product. `compliance-note.md` §4.4 detached "penalty" from export and reattached it to the user's data generally. The CEO's reductio is accepted and the CGO could find no reading that escapes it: **export is worse than the app in every product that has ever shipped one, so a reading on which that gap is itself a penalty is satisfiable by nothing — and a reading no product can satisfy is a wrong reading.** The CEO's further point is adopted: the clause's proper domain is full-dataset export for data-protection and portability purposes, not in-app feature access. Of the three verbs in the original condition — reading, editing, exporting — only the third was ever grounded in this clause.

**C1.2 — "it costs us nothing" used a definition of Cost the Constitution does not use.** Definitions define Cost as *"everything it takes to run a product or the company, itemised — hosting, tooling, support, third-party services, and labour valued at a published market benchmark whether or not it is actually paid"*; Article 2.1 requires *"a fair-market cost for labour (including the founder's, whether or not it is actually drawn)."* Build labour is £14,719, the largest line in the cost model. The CGO's and CFO's argument substituted **marginal** cost for the Constitution's defined **total** cost. **Article 9 names redefining "cost" as a gaming vector**, exposed by the rule that definitions change only by public amendment. It was redefined here in the customer's favour; the CGO records that the direction is not a defence, because Article 9 names the redefinition and not its direction.

**C1.3 — the weight behind the rule was overstated, and no seat raised it.** *"Three seats independently reserved a block"* is wrong. The CFO's block covers *"unpriced or uncosted launches"* and does not reach Article 4; the block reserved at `cost-sheet.md` §8.2 was outside that charter. The UX seat's block is properly grounded. The CGO's was grounded on C1.1. **One block was properly held, not three.**

**C1.4 — a heuristic was treated as an article.** The CFO's test that *"the paywall sits precisely on the cost"* is a good heuristic and it is the CFO's. Article 2.1 constrains the price **level** (~20% target, 30% hard cap, per product); nothing in the Constitution constrains where a paywall sits. Three documents let it operate as law.

**C1.5 — the underlying gap, recorded as a finding about the Constitution rather than about Haunt.** There is no clause about gating content the user authored. Three seats felt its absence and filled it by over-reading a clause about exit. This is the same failure class as objection O10 — no seat re-derives another seat's work — applied to reasoning rather than arithmetic. **Condition 6 is extended accordingly: re-derivation by a second seat covers reasoning, not only arithmetic.**

**C1.6 — why this is classified a correction and not a weakening.** The CGO recommended the WEAKENING label voluntarily, noting that Article 11.2 formally governs amendments to the Constitution and does not reach a gate condition, but that removing a published customer protection without naming it is what Article 9 guards against. The CEO argued it is a correction of a false condition, and the CVO agreed, on three grounds:

1. Condition 8 was a **claim about what the Constitution requires**, and that claim was false. Correcting a misstatement of the rules is not amending them. **Article 4's protections are identical today to what they were on 2026-09-16**; only Candour's description of them has changed.
2. **This record has never been published.** Publication is due 2026-10-16. No customer has seen the rule, nobody has relied on it, and no protection is being taken from anyone. The public record will only ever have said the accurate thing.
3. WEAKENING asserts that Candour reduced a protection it properly owed. It did not — it mistakenly claimed to owe one. **Applying the label anyway would put a false statement in the record pointing the other way, and inaccuracy in the self-critical direction is still inaccuracy** — a failure this record already documents four times.

   **Two acts happened here, and an earlier draft of this correction described one.** Condition 8 was both a false claim about what Article 4 requires *and* a standing product rule Candour voluntarily adopted at its own gate. The first is corrected; the second is **withdrawn**, and it was available to keep on judgment grounds had the CEO wished. Naming only the first would let a withdrawal travel under a correction's cover. *(Skeptic, C1 review: grounds 1 and 3 overclaim; remedy adopted.)*

   **Also recorded, because it costs nothing and the alternative is a bad lesson:** the CGO convicted itself twice, volunteered two errors no one had raised, and recommended the classification least favourable to itself — and was then overruled on that recommendation by the CEO and CVO. A company that wants seats to audit themselves that way should say plainly that it noticed. It noticed.

   What Article 9 actually guards against is **silence**, not the absence of a particular word — the CGO's own phrasing was *"silently improving its own reasoning between decision and publication."* The answer to silence is visibility: the original text is preserved verbatim above, the error is named and attributed, and all five findings are recorded. **Had this correction come after publication, the classification would be different**, and that is stated here so the distinction cannot be borrowed later for a case it does not fit.

**Consequential corrections owed elsewhere** (visibly, in place, not silently): `products/haunt/compliance-note.md` §4.4; `products/haunt/cost-sheet.md` §8.2; `products/haunt/ux-note.md` §6.2 — all three inherit C1.1, C1.2 or C1.4.

**Review status:** this correction is being sent to the Skeptic before publication. Not because 5.3 requires it — the gate has passed — but because Condition 6 of this record institutionalises re-derivation by a second seat, the CGO's re-examination is unreviewed, and the CVO here agrees with the CEO *against* the CGO's own recommendation, which is precisely the configuration a dissent seat exists to check.

# Correction C2 — 2026-09-17, before first publication

**C2.2 — Article 1.2 does not reach a disclosed, non-retroactive read-cap where export is unconditional and complete.** Article 1.2 reads: *"**No exploitation.** We do not profit from confusion, lock-in, addiction, or information asymmetry."* The verb is *"profit **from**"* — the clause prohibits revenue **attributable to** one of four named conditions, not any design that produces a switching cost. "Lock-in" is not in the Definitions, so it carries its ordinary meaning, and the Constitution supplies its own gloss: Article 4's *"Let customers leave with their data"* and Article 7.2's post-shutdown export are this document's operative answer to lock-in. **Lock-in is about exit, and the Constitution's remedy for it is export.** Condition 8.3 delivers that remedy in full. A user who declines to pay leaves with everything. **Article 1.2 forbids profiting from a user's inability to leave; it does not forbid profiting from a user's preference to stay.** All four limbs were checked, not only the cited one: *confusion* is answered by 8.1 and 8.6, *addiction* by 8.4, *information asymmetry* by 8.5 and 8.6 — and on this architecture Candour never holds the journal, so no asymmetry runs between the parties.

**C2.3 — what this does not establish, recorded because the direction runs against the outcome.** The strongest form of the CFO's objection was never that the user cannot leave; it is that Candour would be **withholding**, in its own software, content the user authored and Candour never held. **That is a real harm and it is not lock-in.** Straining Article 1.2 to cover it would repeat the move C1.1 corrected — taking a clause about leaving and making it govern in-app access. **So Article 1.2's failure to reach this is a second, independent confirmation of the gap identified at C1.5**, now recorded in Article 10 of the Constitution: there is no clause about gating content the user authored. **Two clauses have now been tried and neither fits.** If Candour wants the rule binding, the route is unchanged: a public Article 11 amendment, labelled as a strengthening.

# CEO decisions log — post-gate

Decisions taken after the gate, recorded as they are made rather than reconstructed later.

**D1 — Platform: both iOS and Android at launch.** *CEO, 2026-09-20.* Reaffirmed after the CTO surfaced iOS-only as the single largest lever on cost, carrying roughly half the build and half of five years' maintenance. The CEO's original reasoning — that a privacy timeline should not be locked to one platform, and that Google Maps demonstrates Android capability — is unchanged by the cost movement. **What this commits Candour to, recorded so it is not rediscovered later:** the CTO's 2,090-hour build and 360 h/yr maintenance both stand at their two-platform figures; launch is correspondingly further out; and the five-year support commitment at D2 runs on both platforms from launch. The CTO's **Block 2** (no Android MVP resting on undocumented OEM behaviour) remains live and is lifted only by the published device-matrix spike.

**D2 — Support commitment: five years from launch, on both platforms.** *CEO, 2026-09-20.* **What is promised:** for five years from launch the app continues to run on then-current iOS and Android, security and defect fixes are made, and the bundled venue index is refreshed. **What is not promised: new features.** The definition is deliberately matched to what the CTO's 360 h/yr actually funds, so the public promise and the cost model describe the same thing rather than diverging at the first OS release.

**Consequences, recorded because they are easy to forget and impossible to undo.** Constitution v1.2 permits build labour to be amortised only against a *published* commitment, so this unlocks the amortised treatment the CFO's models assume; absent it the £45,957.55 build is a first-year expense and every price roughly triples. The period may be **shortened, never lengthened** (v1.2, Definitions), and any unamortised remainder is written off publicly on discontinuation (Article 7.2). It is **contractual, not marketing** — CRA 2015 s.36(3) makes pre-contract information about characteristics and functionality a term of the contract. The clock starts at **launch**, not today, which with D1's two-platform scope places it further out.

**D3 — Venue matching: nearest-first, with a confirmation prior, and uncertainty shown rather than hidden.** *CEO, 2026-09-20.* Adopts the Engineer's measured remediation (`venue-index-remediation.md`): nearest-first ranking, weighted by the user's own prior confirmations at that location, which more than doubled identity stability (0.31 → 0.67) where duplicate-clustering achieved nothing. **The CEO's addition:** where the match is uncertain, the venue is **marked unconfirmed** in both the timeline and the session rather than silently guessed, and a notification may prompt confirmation.

**Two constraints that travel with it.** Marking a venue unconfirmed in the app is **static, in-context disclosure**, which Condition 8.4 as clarified expressly permits — what 8.4 forbids is interruption and urgency. A *notification* is the interruptive form, so it must be justified as functional capture rather than engagement, kept non-repeating, and it must remain optional: the confirmation surface has to stand alone if notification permission is declined (CGO, requirements item R-equivalent). **Block 4 is not lifted by this decision** — it is lifted by the remediation meeting a bar, and the Engineer failed its own pre-registered 0.8 threshold at 0.67. The one-day name-search measurement that would settle whether the design reaches 81.6% or 100% is still outstanding.

**D4 — Amortisation decoupled from any support promise; Haunt publishes no guaranteed support date.** *CEO, 2026-09-20.* Adopts the CGO's **Option B**: build cost is amortised over a standard company-wide period as a pure accounting convention, and Article 4 gains a duty to **either publish a support date or state plainly that none is given**. Haunt takes the second limb.

**The CEO's reason, recorded in his own terms because it is the right one:** *"what if something happens if I am unable to support a product until the date... if I don't have the time or money?"* A date he cannot guarantee is a promise he should not make, and CRA 2015 s.36(3)–(4) would make it contractually unchangeable once sold against. Saying so plainly is the honest position rather than the weak one. **Customers are not thereby abandoned:** Article 7.2 still guarantees 90 days' notice, free data export through and beyond shutdown, and open-sourcing where third-party rights allow.

**What this supersedes.** **D2 is superseded, not corrected** — nothing in it was false; the framework it relied on changed underneath it. Condition 9.2's *"published five-year support commitment is a precondition"* falls with it. Correction **C5** carries the consequential edits. **The CFO's block does not lift until the amendment is in force:** decoupling without it gives first-year expensing, a £90,205.20 cost base and prices roughly three times those modelled.

**D5 — The sharing layer is not closed; it is gated.** *CEO, 2026-09-20.* Reverses the CVO's decision at the gate to close the door explicitly. The CEO's position: the door should stay open for the case where *"multiple people in a friendship group have it and want to be able to share and request it"* — contingent on time, money, and it being *"genuinely something people want"*, with full due diligence.

**The warning that was given, preserved so it is available at the moment it matters rather than forgotten.** The Research Analyst argued in writing that a sharing layer is the **only growth mechanism this product could ever have**, so the option *"will be exercised for company reasons and then justified with user reasons"*, and that Article 1.1 judges products by unprompted recommendation rather than engineered referral. The CVO accepted that argument at the gate. The CEO has now weighed it and decided otherwise, which is his under 5.4.

**The test that governs reopening, set now while nothing turns on it.** Sharing is proposed only on **evidence of unprompted demand from existing users** — Article 1.1's own test — and never on a growth or revenue argument. It re-enters the pipeline as a **fresh proposal through a full gate**, not as a feature in a build. **The evidence bar is deliberately left undefined, and that is recorded rather than hidden.** *CEO, 2026-09-20: a future problem, and opt-in only.* The CGO found the test as first drafted was toothless — Candour collects nothing by design, so the only available channels are the support inbox and store reviews, both self-selected, and almost anything could clear such a bar. **No standard is set here.** The CVO's "evidence of unprompted demand" states the *direction* of the test, not a threshold that can be passed. **Whoever proposes sharing owns defining the bar, in the proposal, before any evidence is gathered against it** — a threshold set after the evidence arrives is not a threshold. Sharing is in any case opt-in by the CEO's direction.

**The compliance consequence must be on the table from the first line:** on the current architecture Candour is *neither controller nor processor* of journal data; publishing or transmitting reviews makes Candour a **controller**, engaging DPIA, privacy notice, ICO registration and moderation duties, and forfeiting the product's central claim.

**D6 — Standard amortisation period: three years, with a scheduled re-check.** *CEO, 2026-09-21.* Build labour is amortised straight-line over **three years** for every Candour product, as a company-wide accounting convention. Adopted on the CFO's platform-cadence reasoning, retrieved at primary: Apple requires builds on a current SDK annually and Google Play a current target API annually, so by month 36 the original build has been forced through three replacement passes and charging for it beyond that charges customers for an asset that no longer exists.

**The honest residue, recorded rather than smoothed.** The CGO's objection was that the platform evidence establishes a **one-year** cadence, so it brackets the answer at "a small number of years" without selecting three over two — and that the convention is being fixed at the only moment in the company's history when one product exists and its prices are already modelled at two, three and five years. The CFO dropped its Haunt-specific argument unprompted and separated what survives independently. The CGO judged the objection **partly answered, not blocking**. Both seats' positions stand in `pipeline/amortisation-proposal.md` and `pipeline/amendment-draft-amortisation.md`.

**The re-check.** One scheduled revisit at the first product's launch **+ 12 months** — the cheapest evidence that would improve the decision, being a real year of actually running something. **Prospective only:** any change applies to future pricing and can never re-price a customer who has already bought. Article 11.1's rationale requirement and SI 2008/410 Sch 1 para 22(4) both require the period *and its reasons* to be published; the CFO's separation of Haunt-dependent from independent grounds goes into the public change log rather than only into the working paper.

**D7 — The cumulative margin cap binds, with a floor on notice for legacy price rises.** *CEO, 2026-09-21.* Assents to the CGO's determination that *"the cost of serving them"* in Article 2.1 means **that subscriber's own cost** — their single share of the build, plus their own years of running cost — against the CFO's suspicion that the clause was a no-op. On that reading it is a live prohibition: **a long-standing customer's total position can never drift above what a newer one pays.**

**What Candour gives up, stated plainly because it is a real cost.** Grandfathering and legacy tiers as retention levers. Price cuts must reach existing customers; old customers cannot be held on an old higher price while new ones pay less. **What it buys** is a promise that can be checked rather than asserted: staying longer never costs you more.

**The CEO's refinement, which is customer-favourable and compatible.** The cap is one-directional — it forbids drifting *above* what newer customers pay, so a legacy price that is **lower** is not caught by it. Where such a price must rise, the customer gets **at least 90 days' notice, and longer where switching cost is high** — the CEO's example being a complex or deeply integrated application where a user needs more time to move. The 90-day floor aligns with Article 7.2's discontinuation notice on the same reasoning: time to decide and to leave with your data, which stays exportable free throughout. **A floor, not a fixed period.** Consumer law already requires express agreement to vary a contract term (CRA 2015 s.36(4)) and Apple requires consent for subscription rises; this adds Candour's own guaranteed window on top.

**D8 — The confirm interaction: nearest first, options when unclear, search always available.** *CEO, 2026-09-21.* Refines D3 into the interaction itself. **Nearest is offered as the default suggestion. Where the match is uncertain, or several venues overlap, the app shows the options rather than choosing.** A filter and manual search are always reachable, and selection is one easy action throughout.

**Why this answers the Block 4 finding rather than deferring it.** The measured problem was never that the app cannot find a venue; it is that GPS alone ranks the wrong one first in dense areas — modal rank-1 share 0.67, top-five recall 53.1%. **Because the design requires the user to confirm, identity is settled by the person and not by the coordinate** — provided the right venue is *reachable*. The non-circular measurement puts typed retrieval at **100.0%** at 25 m error, 98.0% under the harshest single-query policy and 95.9% on an adverse bound. So reachability is the whole game, and it is a requirements decision.

**Three constraints the measurements attach, all binding at requirements:**

1. **Search is a first-class part of confirming, not a buried fallback.** At five rows retrieval is 93.9% and the Engineer's bar fails; at **twenty reachable rows** it passes. The list must reach twenty.
2. **Prefix matching, not whole-string.** On the 44 venues whose registered and displayed names genuinely differ, typing the full registered name retrieves the right row **50.0%** of the time; typing four characters retrieves it **100%**.
3. **Never auto-select.** 3.6% of typed queries put a name-similar *wrong* venue first — the Engineer's example being `Castle Tea Room` five metres from `Castle Lodge Buttery`. The simulation assumed nobody picks it; the interaction must not make picking it the path of least resistance.

**The CEO's position on the block, recorded because it is a judgment and not a measurement.** *"I do not see this as a major issue… something that can be improved on as we go, using precise location permissions and/or UX."* The CVO's reservation, recorded once: the corruption the spike measured is **silent and permanent** — there is no server, no telemetry and no backfill, so a wrong identity cannot be detected or repaired after shipping. ~~**D8's mitigations meet that reservation** by moving identity from the coordinate to the user.~~

*Corrected 2026-09-21 at the CTO's written disagreement (`block-4-ruling.md` §4). That sentence was the CVO's and it overclaimed.* **A wrong confirmation is exactly as invisible to Candour as a wrong coordinate** — the Engineer's simulation sets the false-pick rate to zero *by assumption* while measuring two hazards that say otherwise: a name-similar wrong venue placed first on **3.6%** of typed queries, and the confirmation prior putting last week's pub first on **57.5%** of visits next door.

**The reservation does have an answer, but it is a different one.** A coordinate-made error is invisible to everyone. A user-made error is invisible to Candour but **visible to the user** — which is why the residual must be *repairable on the device*, and why the CTO added **P5** to the governing bar rather than accepting the Engineer's substitution as a pure relaxation. That answer costs requirement **R-5**, and it carries a tension the PM/BA must resolve: D8's "one easy action" and D3's confirmation prompt must make selection a low-friction **choice**, never low-friction **assent**. **Block 4 is the CTO's and is not lifted by this decision** — it either lifts on the measurement plus D8, or the CEO overrules it under 5.6, which is recorded publicly with reasons.

The slug and codename stay `haunt`.** *CEO, 2026-09-21.* The CVO chose "Haunt" at spark and the CEO's objection was the better reasoning: *"Haunt seems a bit dark."* **Singular reads as a ghost following you; plural reads as the places you frequent** — and for a location-history product, being invisibly followed is precisely the association the product exists to refuse. Shortlisted against **Snug** and **Rounds**; both were rejected on the same ground, that every tagline they support is about a night out, while the product covers 346,184 venues rather than 42,407 pubs. **Haunts is the only one of the three that does not quietly narrow the product to drinking.** Reversible later at no cost; the repository slug, branch names and six commits stay `haunt`.

**D12 — Photos may be fetched from the user's own cloud; the claim is reworded, not the product blocked.** *CEO, 2026-09-22.* Supersedes D11(b)'s "on this phone only" blocking rule and its open download-button question.

**The CEO's argument, adopted:** fetching a photo the user attached, from the user's own iCloud or Google Photos, in order to display it back to them is *"not a blocker as it is up to the user whether they want to upload photos and we are not sending them anywhere, just fetching them."* **The privacy substance holds:** the request runs from the user's device to the user's own storage through the platform photo framework; Candour receives nothing and sends nothing new. **What it breaks is only a sentence.** The marketing statement settled at the proposal's third correction — *"The app makes no other network calls"* — would become false, and it is a contractual term under CRA 2015 s.36(3) and an Article 4 claim that must be substantiable. **So the statement is reworded rather than the feature blocked**, e.g.: *"Haunts never sends your data anywhere. If a photo you attached is stored in iCloud or Google Photos, your phone may download it to show it to you."* No manual download button is required; the fetch is automatic.

**Thumbnail cache at attach time.** A small display-size thumbnail is stored when a photo is attached; the original stays referenced. Something always displays even after the original becomes cloud-only or is deleted, which substantially reduces the thinning-over-time problem D11(b) recorded. Storage impact stays small.

**The distinction this draws, recorded because it governs the next case.** A photo fetch travels from the user to **the user's own** storage. **A map tile request sends the locations a user is viewing to a third-party tile server** — in effect their location history, to someone else. The first is acceptable on the CEO's argument; the second is not settled by it, and the heatmap's map is referred to the CTO on exactly that question.

1. **The constitutional test is time to move, and this product has no workflow to migrate.** The CEO's own example when he set the rule (D7) was *a complex or deeply integrated application*. 2. **90 can be raised later; 180 can never be lowered.** The period *"may be lengthened, never shortened"*, so 90 is the reversible choice. The CFO read the option 90 preserves as an option to shorten; it is an option **not yet to have committed** to the longer figure.
3. **Rises are expected to be rare** — the CFO models Haunts' prices as mostly falling, at the cliff and at each recompute — and **90 days is already 3.3× Apple's 27-day minimum**, given in advance.

The CFO's case for 180 stands on the record in `cost-sheet-v3.md` §10 and is not answered by pretending it was weak: it is declined on the grounds above.

**(c) The privacy wording is approved** as the PM/BA drafted it at `requirements.md` §12.2.8: *"Haunts sends your data nowhere else. If a photo you attached is kept in iCloud Photos or Google Photos, your phone may fetch it from there to show it to you."* It replaces D12's example sentence, which was false whenever backup is on. **The CGO must still confirm it as a contract term** under CRA 2015 s.36(3) before it ships.

**Still open, to be taken one at a time:** ~~the pay-once price stepping down each year as the build is recovered (£25.49, £22.49, £18.95)~~ *(decided at D15)*; ~~the Article 9 shared-fee allocation rule, which the CFO has now raised twice and asks the CEO to adopt or decline in writing~~ *(decided at D16)*; and ~~whether Candour openly absorbs pay-once buyers' ~£2.31 a year support cost after year three~~ *(decided at D17)*.

**D15 — The pay-once price steps down on results, not on the calendar.** *CEO, 2026-09-22.* Declines the CFO's time-based schedule (£28.99 → £25.49 → £22.49 → £18.95 by year): *"it should be based on data, i.e. if we hit ~1/3 target in year one, then step down and so on."*

**The rule, as proposed by the CVO for the CFO to confirm:** the pay-once price steps down **each time a further third of the capitalised build has been recovered from customers**, assessed at each annual cost-sheet review. "Recovered from customers to date" is already a published figure under Constitution v1.3 (Article 7.2 as amended; Article 2.1's amortisation schedule), so the trigger rests on a number anyone can check.

**Two guardrails, binding:**
1. **Article 2.1's 30% cap operates independently of any milestone.** If volume runs ahead and margin exceeds the cap, a cut is compulsory whether or not a third has been recovered. The milestone rule can only make prices fall sooner, never later than the cap requires.
2. **The milestones and the step sizes are published before first sale** and follow the Article 9 row on prices that never fall: a trigger defined after the fact is a price held high by moving the threshold.

**Why it is better than the calendar, recorded because it is the CEO's reasoning and it is sound:** per-unit cost is annual cost divided by units sold, so the honest price is driven by volume; a date is a proxy for volume that is right only if sales track the plan. **What it does not change:** amortisation remains time-based under v1.3, so at month 36 the build leaves the cost base regardless of sales, and the price must reflect that at the next review whether or not the final third has been recovered.

**D16 — Shared company costs are split equally across live products, with a 10% threshold for weighting.** *CEO, 2026-09-22.* Adopts, in writing as the CFO asked on its second raising, the rule for costs that belong to the company rather than to any one product — AI tooling, domains and similar, about **£1,030 a year (2.4% of Haunts' costs)** at present. **Split equally across live products each year.** While Haunts is the only product it carries all of it; its share halves when a second product ships.

**The CEO's threshold, which makes the rule self-limiting:** equal split holds **while a product's share of shared costs moves its price by less than 10%**. **At 10% or more, the allocation must be weighted** — so that a small product is not priced up by costs a large one mostly drives. The weighting basis (revenue, usage or another measure) is **not chosen now**; the CFO proposes it when the threshold is first approached, **and publishes it before it is applied** — an allocation basis chosen after the numbers are known is the same vector Article 9 names for amortisation periods.

**Recorded because it binds every future product, not Haunts alone:** this is a company-wide rule sitting in a product's decision record. It should move into the Constitution or the CFO's standing method at the next amendment, where the next product will find it.

**D17 — Candour absorbs pay-once buyers' support after their payment is used up.** *CEO, 2026-09-22.* A pay-once buyer's £28.99 is recovered over roughly three years; they continue to cost about **£2.31 a year each** in support thereafter. **Candour carries that cost openly**, stated on the cost sheet as a known loss met from Candour's margin. The alternatives were declined because neither is honest: loading it onto subscribers' prices is a hidden cross-subsidy between customers of the kind Article 2.1 exists to prevent, and charging pay-once buyers again would break the premise of the tier they chose.

**D18 — Every price is recomputed at each annual review and cut whenever the numbers allow.** *CEO, 2026-09-22.* Adopts the CFO's recompute rule (`cost-sheet-v3.md` §8.3), which the D15 addendum surfaced as an open decision the customer-facing copy depended on. At each annual cost-sheet review every tier is worked out again from actual costs and volume, and **cut whenever the numbers allow**, not only when Article 2.1's 30% cap forces it. **Consequence:** after month 36 the monthly price can fall to **69p** at the reference volume; without the rule it would fall only to 79p, and only above roughly 7,170 subscribers. It applies the principle the CEO has held throughout — costs fall, prices fall — as a standing obligation rather than a discretion.

**Housekeeping that travels with it, adopted as the CFO recommended:** a **quarterly margin check** (about an hour each, the CFO's estimate) that brings the annual review forward if projected margin passes **25%**, since a mid-year surge caught only at year end cannot be undone; and a **defined financial year** for Haunts, since that is the year the cap is tested over.

# Correction C6 — 2026-09-21, before first publication

**Classification: CORRECTION.** Two statements about what the law requires were wrong. Under the test settled at C1.6 and carried in `pipeline/templates/decision-record.md`, *"a **CORRECTION** fixes a false statement about what the rules require, before first publication."* **The decision at D7 is unaffected and is not reopened; only two of its supporting legal statements are.** Raised and retrieved by the **CGO**, 2026-09-21, at the CEO's own instruction to check the refinement rather than accept it.

**The sentence, as adopted at D7:** *"Consumer law already requires express agreement to vary a contract term (CRA 2015 s.36(4)) and Apple requires consent for subscription rises; this adds Candour's own guaranteed window on top."*

**C6.1 — CRA 2015 s.36(4) does not reach price.** s.36(3) incorporates as contract terms only the information at *"paragraph (a), (j) or (k) of Schedule 1 or paragraph (a), (v) or (w) of Schedule 2"*, glossed in the section itself as *"(main characteristics, functionality and compatibility)"*. **Price is Schedule 2 paragraph (f)** — *"the total price of the goods, services or digital content inclusive of taxes…"* — **which s.36(3) does not incorporate.** s.36 is headed *"Digital content to be as described"*; it governs description, not price. [Retrieved at primary 2026-09-21: legislation.gov.uk, CRA 2015 s.36 and SI 2013/3134 Schedule 2.]

**What this does *not* disturb, stated precisely so the correction is not over-read.** s.36(3)–(4) **does** reach a published **support date**, which is main-characteristics and functionality information. **So Condition 9.2's citation stands, D2's stands, D4's stands, and Article 4's new support duty is correctly grounded.** The error is confined to applying a description provision to a pricing question.

**C6.2 — what actually governs a price rise, and what Apple actually requires.** **UK law's protection here runs through cancellation rather than consent.** CRA 2015 Schedule 2 — an indicative list of *"Consumer contract terms which **may** be regarded as unfair"*, which confers no right on anyone — includes at paragraph 15 *"a term which has the object or effect of permitting a trader to increase the price… **without giving the consumer the right to cancel the contract** if the final price is too high"*. Paragraph 23 takes a variation term on a contract of **indeterminate duration** outside **paragraphs 11, 12 and 14** where the trader gives reasonable notice and the consumer is free to dissolve — **and paragraph 23 does not reach paragraph 15.** **"Reasonable" carries no number.** [Retrieved at primary 2026-09-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/schedule/2 and .../paragraph/23.]

**Apple requires consent only in three narrow cases** — a consent-requiring region; a rise *"more than 50% of the current price"* **and** exceeding *"approximately US$5 per period for non-annual subscriptions, or US$50 per year for annual subscriptions"*; or a rise within the previous 12 months. Otherwise *"Apple will automatically notify subscribers of the price increase with no additional request for consent"*, with notice **27 days** before renewal for a monthly plan — and, in the no-consent case, *"**after** the new price goes into effect."* [Retrieved at primary 2026-09-21: App Store Connect Help, managing pricing for auto-renewable subscriptions. **Single source by nature** — Apple is the only publisher of Apple's rules.]

**Applied to Haunt: no price rise Candour would plausibly make reaches the consent thresholds.** On the monthly tier a 50% rise on 99p is about 50p against a US$5 gate — roughly ten times out of reach. On the annual tier the limb needs **both** >50% **and** >~US$50, and no realistic rise on £9.89 clears the second. *(Stated at that strength deliberately: an earlier draft said £9.89 "could be quintupled without approaching US$50", which re-derives to a difference of £39.56, roughly US$47–53 — borderline, not impossible. Apple also publishes a **per-storefront thresholds table** which **no seat has opened**; the GB figure is a published number rather than an FX conversion, and it is flagged unretrieved rather than assumed.)* **On an ordinary first price rise, Apple requires no consent and gives 27 days' notice before the subscriber's renewal.**

**C6.3 — a third citation, corrected in the same pass because it is the same class of error.** Condition 9.4 states *"Article 2.3.3.1 lands the cut on the installed base."* **The obligation to cut the price when the build leaves the cost base comes from Article 2.1** — the ~20% target and the 30% cap, measured per financial year, which admits no justification. **Article 2.3.3.1 is why a reduction reaches existing customers first; Article 2.1 is why there must be a reduction at all.** The point 9.4 makes is right; the clause it hangs on is the wrong one. Changes no number and no decision.

**C6.4 — the direction of both errors, recorded because it runs *toward* the decision rather than away from it.** Both of D7's mistaken premises made the CEO's own refinement look like a courtesy layered on two existing guarantees. **Corrected, it is the substantial protection in the stack at Candour's price points: 90 days against a platform notice period of 27, in a legal regime that specifies no number at all. The refinement is not redundant.**

**One claim made in the course of reaching that conclusion is withdrawn, and it was the CGO's own.** A draft of this correction said Apple's notice in the no-consent case is sent *"after the new price has taken effect"*. **That sentence is on Apple's page and was quoted accurately — and three others in the same section say the opposite**, including *"Subscribers receive notice of a price change 27 days before the renewal date"*, the phrase *"the minimum required notice period"*, and a timing table whose first row is *"27 days before renewal date"*. **Read whole, "goes into effect" means the storefront, not the customer: no Apple subscriber is charged the higher price before being told.** The multiple of 3.3× survives; **the advance-versus-retrospective contrast does not, and is withdrawn before publication rather than after.** Found by the Skeptic under gate Condition 6 — with the contradicting text inside this seat's own retrieval, which is what makes it worth recording rather than quietly fixing.

**C6.5 — D6's recorded reason carries the same unsupported Apple claim, and needs the same fix. This is not a reopening of D6.** D6 records the period as *"adopted on the CFO's platform-cadence reasoning, retrieved at primary: Apple requires builds on a current SDK annually and Google Play a current target API annually, so by month 36 the original build has been forced through three replacement passes."* **The Apple page states one requirement with one date and asserts no cadence**, and the CFO's second date — 29 April 2024, not present on the cited page — describes a **24-month** interval. **Google's requirement is annual; Apple's is not, on the retrievable evidence.** The decision stands; the sentence recording its reason does not. **Replace it with what holds**, in the terms adopted in `CHANGELOG.md` v1.3 item 2: Google's annual target-API requirement; Apple's current-SDK floor with no stated cadence; neither compelling a rebuild of code already shipped; the company-wide reason thinner than three years needs; **Haunt's React Native shared codebase as corroboration for this product that does not generalise to a future iOS-only native product**; and three years recorded as **a judgment inside a bracket the evidence does not close**, which is why the re-check at launch + 12 months exists. Under Article 11.1 the rationale is part of the amendment, and under SI 2008/410 Sch 1 para 22(4) — the precedent Candour chose to borrow — **the reasons are what must be published. A sound decision resting on a misstated reason still publishes a misstatement.**

**C6.6 — and the refinement as phrased at D7 would have bound nothing, which the CEO asked to have checked.** *"Longer where switching cost is high"* carried no definition, no test, no number, no owner and no moment of decision. **Article 4 as adopted fixes that with the structure this Constitution already uses twice:** the period is chosen **per product, published before the first sale, and may be lengthened but never shortened** — so it cannot be tuned once a rise is on the table — and the test is anchored to something checkable: **the time a competent user would need to move, judged from the export the product actually ships.** *(Where a product's export is not directly loadable by any alternative Candour is aware of, the CGO's judgment is 180 days rather than 90.
